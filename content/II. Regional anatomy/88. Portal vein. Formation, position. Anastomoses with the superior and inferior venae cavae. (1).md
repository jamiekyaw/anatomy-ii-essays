> Portal vein. Formation, position. Anastomoses with the superior and inferior venae cavae.

---

# 1. Formation
- **Formed by:** The union of the **Splenic vein** and the **Superior Mesenteric Vein (SMV)**.
- **Location:** Behind the **neck of the pancreas**, at the level of L2 vertebra.

![](https://ars.els-cdn.com/content/image/3-s2.0-B9780128042540000107-f10-25-9780128042540.jpg)

# 2. Position & Course
- **Length:** Approximately 8 cm long.
- **In the Lesser Omentum:** It travels inside the **hepatoduodenal ligament**.
- **The Portal Triad:** It lies **posterior** to the Common Bile Duct and the Hepatic Artery.
- **Termination:** Enters the liver at the **Porta Hepatis**, where it divides into Right and Left branches.

# 3. Porto-Caval Anastomoses (The "Big 3")
These are connections between the Portal system and the Systemic system (Vena Cava). They become enlarged in **Portal Hypertension**.

| Location | Portal Vein Side | Systemic Vein (Caval) Side | Clinical Result |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Lower Esophagus** | Left Gastric Vein | Esophageal veins (Azygos) | **Esophageal Varices** (can cause fatal bleeding) |
| **Anal Canal** | Superior Rectal Vein | Middle & Inferior Rectal Veins | **Hemorrhoids** (Internal) |
| **Umbilicus** | Paraumbilical Veins | Superficial Epigastric Veins | **Caput Medusae** (radiating veins on belly) |

# Summary for Examiners:
- **Formation:** Splenic + Superior Mesenteric Veins.
- **Relation:** Posterior to the pancreas neck and posterior to the bile duct in the lesser omentum.
- **Clinical:** If the liver is blocked (Cirrhosis), blood backs up at the esophagus, rectum, and umbilicus.

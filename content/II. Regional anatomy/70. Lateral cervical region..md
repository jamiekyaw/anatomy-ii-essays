> Lateral cervical region.

---

# 1. Boundaries (The Frame)
- **Anterior:** Posterior border of the **Sternocleidomastoid (SCM)** muscle.
- **Posterior:** Anterior border of the **Trapezius** muscle.
- **Inferior (Base):** Middle 1/3 of the **Clavicle**.
- **Roof:** Deep cervical fascia (investing layer) and skin.
- **Floor:** Muscles (Splenius capitis, Levator scapulae, and Scalene muscles).

# 2. Major Subdivisions
The **Omohyoid muscle** crosses the bottom of the triangle, splitting it into:
1.  **Occipital Triangle** (Large, upper part).
2.  **Omoclavicular / Subclavian Triangle** (Small, lower part).

# 3. High-Yield Contents (The "Must-Knows")
If you remember nothing else, remember these:

- **Spinal Accessory Nerve (CN XI):** 
    - Runs superficial/across the middle of the triangle.
    - **Function:** Supplies the Sternocleidomastoid and Trapezius.
- **Cervical Plexus (Sensory branches):** 
    - They all emerge from the **"Nerve Point of the Neck"** (Erb’s point) at the midpoint of the SCM muscle.
- **Brachial Plexus (Roots/Trunks):** 
    - Passes between the Anterior and Middle Scalene muscles.
    - Provides nerve supply to the upper limb.
- **Phrenic Nerve:** 
    - Lies on the surface of the **Anterior Scalene** muscle.
    - **Function:** Controls the Diaphragm ("C3, 4, 5 keep the diaphragm alive").
- **External Jugular Vein (EJV):** 
    - Crosses superficially over the SCM into the triangle.
- **Subclavian Artery (3rd part):** 
    - Located deep in the lower part of the triangle; used to feel a pulse or stop bleeding in the arm.

# 4. Summary for the Examiner
- "The lateral region is bounded by the **SCM**, **Trapezius**, and **Clavicle**."
- "The most important nerve crossing it is the **Spinal Accessory Nerve (CN XI)**."
- "The **Brachial Plexus** and **Subclavian Artery** pass through the floor between the scalene muscles."

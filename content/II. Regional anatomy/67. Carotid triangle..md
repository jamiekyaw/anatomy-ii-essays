> Carotid triangle.

---

# 1. Boundaries (The Three Borders)
Imagine a triangle in the upper neck:
- **Superior:** Posterior belly of the Digastric muscle.
- **Anterior/Inferior:** Superior belly of the Omohyoid muscle.
- **Posterior:** Anterior border of the Sternocleidomastoid muscle (SCM).

# 2. Main Contents (The "Big Three")
The most important structures located here are:
1. **Common Carotid Artery:** Usually bifurcates (splits) into the Internal and External carotid arteries at the level of **C4**.
2. **Internal Jugular Vein (IJV):** Runs vertically through the triangle.
3. **Vagus Nerve (CN X):** Lies between the artery and the vein.

# 3. Other Contents (Bonus Points)
- **Hypoglossal Nerve (CN XII):** Crosses the triangle horizontally at the top.
- **Ansa Cervicalis:** A nerve loop sitting on top of the carotid sheath.
- **External Carotid Branches:** Specifically the Superior Thyroid, Lingual, and Facial arteries.

# 4. Special Structures (Very High Yield)
- **Carotid Sinus:** A dilation at the base of the internal carotid. It is a **Baroreceptor** (senses blood pressure).
- **Carotid Body:** A small mass at the bifurcation. It is a **Chemoreceptor** (senses oxygen/CO2 levels).

***

**Summary for the Examiner:**
*"The carotid triangle is bounded by the digastric, omohyoid, and sternocleidomastoid muscles. Its main contents are the common carotid artery, the internal jugular vein, and the vagus nerve. It is clinically important because it contains the carotid sinus for blood pressure regulation and is where you palpate the carotid pulse."*

> The superior and inferior vena cava. Position. Main tributaries. Anastomoses between the two caval veins.

---

# 1. Superior Vena Cava (SVC)
- **Position:** Located in the superior mediastinum (upper chest), on the right side.
- **Formation:** Formed by the union of the **Left and Right Brachiocephalic veins**.
- **Termination:** Drains into the upper part of the **Right Atrium**.
- **Main Tributary:** The **Azygos vein** (enters the SVC just before it joins the heart).

# 2. Inferior Vena Cava (IVC)
- **Position:** Located in the posterior abdominal wall, to the **right of the Abdominal Aorta**.
- **Formation:** Formed by the union of the **Left and Right Common Iliac veins** (at level L5).
- **Termination:** Drains into the lower part of the **Right Atrium**.
- **Main Tributaries:**
    1. **Hepatic veins** (from the liver).
    2. **Renal veins** (from the kidneys).
    3. **Right Gonadal vein** (the left one usually drains into the renal vein first).
    4. **Lumbar veins**.

# 3. Caval-Caval Anastomoses
These are "backup routes."
If the IVC or SVC is blocked, blood can still reach the heart through these connections:

- **Azygos/Hemiazygos System:**
	- Connects the IVC (via lumbar veins) to the SVC.
- **Thoracoepigastric Vein:**
	- A superficial connection on the side of the trunk connecting the Axillary vein or the Superficial Epigastric vein.
- **Vertebral Venous Plexus:**
	- A network of veins inside and outside the spinal column that connects the two systems.
- **Deep Abdominal Veins:**
	- Connections between the Epigastric veins on the front of the belly.

---

**Summary for the Examiner:**
- **SVC** is formed by **Brachiocephalic veins**; its main tributary is the **Azygos vein**.
- **IVC** is formed by **Common Iliac veins**; its main tributaries are **Hepatic and Renal veins**.
- They are connected mainly by the **Azygos system** and **Thoracoepigastric veins**.

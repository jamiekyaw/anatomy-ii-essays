> Axillary fossa. Location, structure, content.

---

# 1. Location
The axillary fossa is a pyramid-shaped space located between the upper part of the **chest wall** (thorax) and the **upper arm** (humerus). It acts as a "gateway" for nerves and vessels traveling from the neck to the arm.

# 2. Structure (Boundaries)
Think of it as a 4-sided pyramid:

- **Apex (Top):** The "entrance" formed by the clavicle, 1st rib, and upper edge of the scapula.
- **Base (Bottom):** The skin and axillary fascia (the hollow of the armpit).
- **Anterior Wall (Front):** Pectoralis major and minor muscles.
- **Posterior Wall (Back):** Latissimus dorsi, Subscapularis, and Teres major muscles.
- **Medial Wall (Inner):** Serratus anterior muscle and the upper ribs.
- **Lateral Wall (Outer):** The intertubercular (bicipital) groove of the **humerus**.

# 3. Content
These are the most important structures found inside:

1. **Axillary Artery:** Supplies blood to the upper limb.
2. **Axillary Vein:** Drains blood from the upper limb.
3. **Brachial Plexus (Cords):** The network of nerves that control the arm.
4. **Axillary Lymph Nodes:** Crucial for draining lymph from the breast and arm (very important for clinical exams).
5. **Axillary Fat/Connective Tissue:** Fills the remaining space.

> Large intestine. Embryonic development. Parts. Caecum and colon. Vermiform appendix Macroscopic, microscopic, ultramicroscopic and functional characteristics. Blood and nerve supply.

---

# 1. Embryonic Development
- **Midgut origin:**
	- Caecum, Appendix, Ascending colon, and proximal 2/3 of Transverse colon. (Supplied by **Superior Mesenteric Artery**).
- **Hindgut origin:**
	- Distal 1/3 of Transverse colon, Descending colon, Sigmoid, and Rectum. (Supplied by **Inferior Mesenteric Artery**).

- **Key Event:** Physiological herniation and 270° counter-clockwise rotation around the SMA.

---

# 2. Macroscopic Characteristics (Gross Anatomy)
The large intestine is distinguished from the small intestine by:
1. **Teniae Coli:** Three longitudinal bands of smooth muscle.
2. **Haustra:** Pouches caused by the teniae being shorter than the gut tube.
3. **Omental Appendages:** Small pouches of peritoneum filled with fat.

**Parts:**
- **Caecum:** Blind pouch below the ileocaecal valve.
- **Appendix:** Narrow, worm-like tube; contains lymphoid tissue.
- **Colon:** Ascending, Transverse, Descending, Sigmoid.

# 3. Microscopic (Histology)
- **General Wall:** Four layers (Mucosa, Submucosa, Muscularis Externa, Serosa).
- **Mucosa:** 
    - **NO villi** and no plicae circulares (distinguishes it from the small intestine).
    - **Crypts of Lieberkühn:** Long, straight, tubular glands.
    - **Cell types:** Predominantly **Goblet cells** and **Absorptive cells**.
- **Muscularis Externa:** Outer longitudinal layer is bundled into the three **Teniae Coli**.
- **Appendix Histology:** Characterized by a "ring" of **lymphatic nodules** in the submucosa and a narrow, often star-shaped lumen.

# 4. Ultramicroscopic (Electron Microscope)
- **Enterocytes:** Have short, irregular **microvilli** (striated border) for water absorption.
- **Tight Junctions:** Highly developed to prevent "leakage" of bacteria/toxins into the blood.
- **Mitochondria:** Abundant near the apical surface to provide ATP for active transport of sodium.

---

# 5. Functional Characteristics
1. **Absorption:** Primarily water and electrolytes.
2. **Microbiome:** Synthesis of **Vitamin K** and B-complex vitamins by gut bacteria.
3. **Lubrication:** Massive mucus secretion (Goblet cells) to facilitate passage of feces.
4. **Formation/Storage:** Compaction of indigestible residue into feces.

---

# 6. Blood Supply
- **Midgut derivatives:** 
    - **Superior Mesenteric Artery (SMA):** Ileocolic, Right Colic, and Middle Colic arteries.
- **Hindgut derivatives:** 
    - **Inferior Mesenteric Artery (IMA):** Left Colic, Sigmoid, and Superior Rectal arteries.
- **Venous Drainage:** Via SMA and IMA veins into the **Portal Vein** (to the liver).

---

# 7. Nerve Supply
- **Enteric Nervous System:** Myenteric (Auerbach’s) plexus for motility; Submucosal (Meissner’s) plexus for secretions.
- **Parasympathetic (Stimulates):**
    - **Vagus Nerve (CN X)**
    - **Pelvic Splanchnic Nerves (S2-S4)**
- **Sympathetic (Inhibits):**
    - Thoracic and Lumbar splanchnic nerves (**T10–L2**).
- **Sensory:** Visceral pain fibers follow sympathetic nerves back to the spinal cord (referred pain to the umbilical or hypogastric regions).

# Summary for Quick Revision
- **Is there a villus?** No $\rightarrow$ Large Intestine.
- **Are there fat tags?** Yes $\rightarrow$ Epiploic appendages.
- **Where does it change blood supply?** 2/3 across the Transverse colon.
- **Why so many Goblet cells?** To move dry poop.

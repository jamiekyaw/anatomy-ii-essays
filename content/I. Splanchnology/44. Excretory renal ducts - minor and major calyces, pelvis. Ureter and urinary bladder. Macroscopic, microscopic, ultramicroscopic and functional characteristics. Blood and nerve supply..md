> Excretory renal ducts - minor and major calyces, pelvis. Ureter and urinary bladder. Macroscopic, microscopic, ultramicroscopic and functional characteristics. Blood and nerve supply.

---

# 1. General Histological Plan (The "Urinary Blueprint")
All excretory ducts from the calyces to the bladder share a similar 3-layered wall:
1. **Mucosa:** Transitional epithelium (**Urothelium**) + Lamina propria.
2. **Muscularis:** Smooth muscle (Inner longitudinal, Outer circular—*Note: opposite of the GI tract*).
3. **Adventitia:** Loose connective tissue.

---

# 2. Minor & Major Calyces and Renal Pelvis
- **Macroscopic:** 
    - Minor calyces surround the renal papillae (urine exit).
    - 2–3 minor calyces join to form a Major calyx.
    - Major calyces join to form the **Renal Pelvis**.
- **Microscopic:** Thin urothelium; minimal smooth muscle.
- **Functional:** Act as a funnel to collect urine from the collecting ducts and channel it into the ureter.

# 3. The Ureter
- **Macroscopic:** 25–30 cm long tubes. 
    - **3 Physiological Constrictions:** 
        1. Ureteropelvic junction.
        2. Crossing the iliac vessels (pelvic brim).
        3. Ureterovesical junction (entry into bladder).
- **Microscopic:**
    - **Urothelium:** 3–5 layers thick.
    - **Muscularis:** Upper 2/3 (2 layers); Lower 1/3 (3 layers: inner long., middle circ., outer long.).
- **Functional:** **Peristalsis**

# 4. The Urinary Bladder
- **Macroscopic:** 
    - **Trigone:** Smooth triangular area at the base (2 ureteric orifices + 1 internal urethral orifice).
    - **Rugae:** Folds in the mucosa that disappear when full.
- **Microscopic:**
    - **Urothelium:** Up to 6+ layers thick.
    - **Muscularis:** Very thick, called the **Detrusor Muscle**. 3 intertwined layers.
- **Functional:** Temporary reservoir for urine.

# 5. The Urothelium (Ultramicroscopic Characteristics)
- **Umbrella Cells:** Large, dome-shaped cells on the surface.
- **Asymmetric Unit Membranes (Plaques):** Rigid protein thickened areas (Uroplakins) on the apical surface.
- **Discoid Vesicles:** Infolded "hinges" of membrane. 
    - *When bladder is empty:* Vesicles are inside the cell. 
    - *When bladder is full:* Vesicles unfold and integrate into the surface to increase surface area (like an accordion).
- **Tight Junctions:** Create an impermeable barrier to toxic urine.

---

# 6. Blood and Nerve Supply

## Blood Supply
- **Calyces/Pelvis:** Renal arteries.
- **Ureter:** Segmental supply (Renal, Gonadal, Common Iliac, and Vesical arteries).
- **Bladder:** Superior and Inferior Vesical arteries (branches of Internal Iliac).
- **Venous:** Corresponding veins into the Inferior Vena Cava.

## Nerve Supply (Autonomic)
- **Sympathetic (T11–L2):** Relaxes the detrusor muscle, contracts the internal sphincter (**Storage**).
- **Parasympathetic (S2–S4 / Pelvic Splanchnic):** Contracts the detrusor, relaxes the internal sphincter (**Voiding/Micturition**).
- **Sensory:** GVA fibers detect "stretch" and signal the brain when the bladder is full.

# Summary Checklist for the Exam:
1. **Urothelium =** Transitional epithelium.
2. **Umbrella cells + Plaques =** The barrier/expansion mechanism.
3. **Reverse muscle layers =** Inner longitudinal/Outer circular (Ureter).
4. **Detrusor =** Bladder muscle.
5. **Trigone =** Smooth, sensitive, embryologically different.
6. **Peristalsis =** How urine moves in the ureter.

# I. The Thymus
The thymus is a **primary lymphoid organ** where T-lymphocyte precursors (from bone marrow) mature and become "immunocompetent."

### 1. Macroscopic Characteristics
- **Location:** Anterior superior mediastinum, posterior to the sternum.
- **Structure:** Bilobed (two lobes) connected by areolar tissue.
- **Age Changes (Involution):** It is most active in childhood. After puberty, it undergoes **thymic involution**, where lymphatic tissue is largely replaced by adipose (fat) tissue.
- **Capsule:** Surrounded by a thin connective tissue capsule.

![|500](https://nci-media.cancer.gov/pdq/media/images/729194.jpg)

### 2. Microscopic Characteristics
The thymus is divided into many **incomplete lobules** by connective tissue **septa** (trabeculae) extending from the capsule. Each lobule has two zones:

- **A. Cortex (Outer, Darker):**
    - Packed with small, developing **T-lymphocytes (thymocytes)**.
    - **Epithelial Reticular Cells (ERCs) Types I, II, III:** Form a framework.
- **B. Medulla (Inner, Lighter):**
    - Contains fewer lymphocytes and more ERCs (Types IV, V, VI).
    - **Hassall’s Corpuscles:** The "hallmark" of the thymus. They are concentric whorls of Type VI ERCs that may be keratinized.

![|500](https://embryology.med.unsw.edu.au/embryology/images/thumb/f/ff/Thymus_histology_06.jpg/600px-Thymus_histology_06.jpg)

### 3. Blood-Thymus Barrier
**Definition:** A physiological barrier in the **cortex** that prevents circulating antigens from reaching developing T-cells (which would cause them to die prematurely).
- **Components (3 Layers):**
    1. **Endothelium**
    2. **Basal Lamina**
    3. **Type I ERCs**
- *Note:* There is NO barrier in the medulla.

![|500](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK546601/bin/Blood_Thymus_Barrier_2-01.jpg)

### 4. Blood and Nerve Supply
- **Arterial Supply:**
	- Internal thoracic artery
	- inferior thyroid arteries
- **Venous Drainage:**
	- Drains into the left brachiocephalic
	- internal thoracic
	- inferior thyroid veins.
- **Nerve Supply:**
	- Parasympathetic (Vagus nerve)
	- Sympathetic (Stellate ganglion).
	- The nerves primarily regulate blood vessel diameter.

# II. Bone Marrow
The bone marrow is the primary site of **hematopoiesis** (blood cell formation) in adults.

### 1. Microscopic Characteristics
There are two main types:
- **Red Bone Marrow:**
	- Haematopoietically active.
	- Found in the epiphyses of long bones and in flat bones (sternum, iliac crest).
- **Yellow Bone Marrow:**
	- Inactive.
	- Dominated by **adipocytes** (fat).
	- Can revert to red marrow in cases of severe blood loss.

![](https://embryology.med.unsw.edu.au/embryology/images/1/1e/Bone_marrow_histology_01.jpg)

**Structural Components:**
- **Stroma:**
	- The “support frame” of the bone marrow that holds everything in place.
- **Hematopoietic cords (islands):**
	- Little groups of developing blood cells growing and maturing.
- **Sinusoids:**
	- Big, leaky blood vessels
	- Finished blood cells leave the marrow and enter the bloodstream.

### 2. Ultramicroscopic Characteristics (Fine Structure)
Under an electron microscope, we see specific arrangements:
- **Sinusoid wall:**
	- The blood vessels in the bone marrow have a loose, leaky wall
	- Cells can pass through easily.
- **Megakaryocytes:**
	- These are huge cells that sit next to the vessels
	- "Shed” little pieces that become platelets, which go into the blood.
- **Macrophages:**
	- Eat up leftover junk, like old nuclei from developing red blood cells.

![|500](https://media.springernature.com/m685/springer-static/image/art%3A10.1007%2Fs10456-017-9577-2/MediaObjects/10456_2017_9577_Fig1_HTML.gif)

### 3. Functional Characteristics
1. **Production (Hematopoiesis):** Continuous supply of Erythrocytes, Leukocytes, and Platelets.
2. **Phagocytosis & Filtration:** Macrophages in the marrow destroy malformed blood cells.
3. **Iron Storage:** Macrophages store iron (as ferritin or hemosiderin) from broken-down hemoglobin to reuse it for new RBCs.
4. **Immunological:** Site of **B-lymphocyte maturation** (The "B" in B-cell stands for Bone Marrow).

> Arteries. Microscopic, ultramicroscopic and functional characteristics of the arterial wall. Classification.

---

# 1. General Characteristics
Arteries are blood vessels that carry blood **away from the heart** toward the capillary beds. Because they operate under high hydrostatic pressure, their walls are significantly thicker than those of veins.

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/35/Artery.svg)

## The Three Tunics (General Wall Structure)
The arterial wall consists of three concentric layers:

1. **Tunica Intima (Inner layer):**
    - **Endothelium:** Simple squamous epithelium.
    - **Subendothelial layer:** Loose connective tissue (allows for movement).
    - **Internal Elastic Lamina (IEL):** A sheet of elastin with fenestrations (holes) to allow nutrients to diffuse to deeper layers.
2. **Tunica Media (Middle layer):**
    - **Components:** Circularly arranged smooth muscle cells, elastic fibers, and collagen.
    - **Significance:** Usually the **thickest layer** in arteries. It controls the diameter of the vessel (vasoconstriction/vasodilation).
    - **External Elastic Lamina (EEL):** Separates the media from the adventitia (more prominent in muscular arteries).
3. **Tunica Adventitia (Outer layer):**
    - **Components:** Collagen and elastic fibers (longitudinal).
    - **Vasa Vasorum:** "Vessels of the vessels." Small blood vessels that supply the outer wall of large arteries.
    - **Nervi Vasorum:** Autonomic nerves (mostly sympathetic) that control smooth muscle contraction.

# 2. Ultramicroscopic & Functional Features
To pass "ultramicroscopic" questions, you must mention specific cellular details visible only under an Electron Microscope (EM):

- **Endothelial Cells:**
    - **Weibel-Palade Bodies:** Rod-like inclusions containing **Von Willebrand Factor** (clotting) and P-selectin.
    - **Tight Junctions (Zonula occludens):** Prevent leakage between cells.
    - **Pinocytotic Vesicles:** Transport substances across the blood-tissue barrier.
- **Smooth Muscle Cells (SMC):**
    - Connected by **Gap Junctions** to allow coordinated contraction.
    - **Dual Function:** In arteries, SMCs are the only cells in the media; they synthesize collagen, elastin, and ground substance (a fibroblast-like role).
- **Elastic Lamellae:** Appear as wavy, fenestrated sheets that allow for expansion and recoil.

# 3. Classification of Arteries
Arteries are classified based on their size and the predominant tissue in the Tunica Media.

![](https://s3-us-west-2.amazonaws.com/courses-images-archive-read-only/wp-content/uploads/sites/403/2015/04/21031420/2103_Muscular_and_Elastic_Artery_Arteriole.jpg)

## A. Elastic Arteries (Large / Conducting Arteries)
- **Examples:** Aorta, Pulmonary artery, Brachiocephalic, Common Carotid.
- **Histology:** Tunica media contains 40–70 layers of **fenestrated elastic membranes** interspersed with smooth muscle.
- **Functional Feature (Windkessel Effect):**
    - **Systole:** They expand to store the stroke volume.
    - **Diastole:** They recoil to push blood forward, ensuring continuous blood flow even when the heart is resting.

## B. Muscular Arteries (Medium / Distributing Arteries)
- **Examples:** Radial, Femoral, Brachial, Splenic.
- **Histology:**
    - Tunica media is dominated by **smooth muscle cells** (up to 40 layers).
    - **Distinct Internal Elastic Lamina (IEL):** Appears as a bright, wavy line.
    - **Distinct External Elastic Lamina (EEL):** Clearly visible between media and adventitia.
- **Functional Feature:** They regulate blood flow to specific organs via vasoconstriction and vasodilation.

## C. Arterioles (Small / Resistance Vessels)
- **Definition:** Diameter < 0.1 mm.
- **Histology:**
    - Tunica intima is very thin; IEL may be absent in the smallest arterioles.
    - Tunica media consists of **1 to 2 layers** of smooth muscle.
    - Tunica adventitia is very thin and blends with surrounding CT.
- **Functional Feature:** They provide the **greatest resistance** to blood flow. They are the primary determinants of systemic blood pressure.

# 4. Functional Characteristics of the Arterial Wall
1. **Elasticity:** Allows vessels to handle high pressure (compliance).
2. **Contractility:** Sympathetic stimulation causes vasoconstriction, increasing blood pressure.
3. **Metabolic Activity:** Endothelial cells produce **Nitric Oxide (NO)** for vasodilation and **Endothelin** for vasoconstriction.
4. **Barrier Function:** Semi-permeable membrane regulating the exchange of fluid and macromolecules.

- **Resistance vessels** (Term for arterioles).
- **Internal Elastic Lamina** (Term for muscular arteries).

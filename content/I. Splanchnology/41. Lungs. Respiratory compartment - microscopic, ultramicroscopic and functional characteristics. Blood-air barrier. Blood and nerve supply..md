> Lungs. Respiratory compartment - microscopic, ultramicroscopic and functional characteristics. Blood-air barrier. Blood and nerve supply.

---

# 1. The Respiratory Compartment
This is the **exchange zone** where gas actually moves into blood. Everything before this is just the "conduction zone."
- **Pathway:**
	- Respiratory bronchioles $\rightarrow$ Alveolar ducts $\rightarrow$ Alveolar sacs $\rightarrow$ Alveoli.
- **Alveoli:** The functional unit. Roughly 300 million per lung.

#### Microscopic / Ultramicroscopic Cells:
- **Type I Pneumocytes (95% of surface):**
	- Simple squamous
	- Extremely thin for gas exchange. Cannot divide.
- **Type II Pneumocytes (5% of surface):**
	- Cuboidal
	    - **Function 1:** Produce **Surfactant** helps to prevent alveolar collapse by reducing surface tension.
	    - **Function 2:** Stem cells to replace damaged Type I cells.
	    - Contains **Lamellar bodies** (storage for surfactant).
- **Alveolar Macrophages**
	- Clean up debris/bacteria.

---

# 2. The Blood-Air Barrier (Respiratory Membrane)
The physical barrier gas must cross between the alveolar air and the capillary blood. Extremely thin (0.2 – 0.6 μm).

**The 4 Layers (Inside out):**
1. **Surfactant layer**
	- Lining the alveolus.
2. **Type I Pneumocyte**
	- Epithelium and its basement membrane.
3. **Fused Basal Lamina**
	- Where the epithelium and capillary membranes stick together.
4. **Capillary Endothelial cell** and its basement membrane.

---

# 3. Blood Supply (Dual Circulation)
The lungs have two separate blood systems:

1. **Pulmonary Circulation:**
    - **Route:** Right Ventricle $\rightarrow$ Pulmonary Artery $\rightarrow$ Alveolar Capillaries $\rightarrow$ Pulmonary Veins $\rightarrow$ Left Atrium.
    - **Purpose:** To oxygenate deoxygenated blood.
    - **Pressure:** Low pressure.

2. **Bronchial Circulation:**
    - **Route:** Thoracic Aorta $\rightarrow$ Bronchial Arteries.
    - **Purpose:** To supply oxygen/nutrients to the **lung tissue itself** (bronchi, connective tissue).
    - **Note:** Most bronchial venous blood drains into pulmonary veins (a "physiological shunt").

---

# 4. Nerve Supply
Controlled by the **Pulmonary Plexus** at the root of the lung.

- **Parasympathetic (Vagus Nerve - CN X):**
	- Bronchoconstriction, increases secretion, vasodilation.
- **Sympathetic (Sympathetic Trunk):**
    - Bronchodilation, inhibits secretion, vasoconstriction.
- **Visceral Afferent:** Sensory fibers (pain, cough reflex, stretch receptors).

> Heart – microscopic, ultramicroscopic and functional characteristics of the heart wall. Nerve and blood supply.

---

# 1. Layers of the Heart Wall (Microscopic)
The heart wall consists of three distinct layers:

- **Endocardium (Inner):**
    - Lines the chambers and covers the valves.
    - Consists of **endothelium** (simple squamous epithelium) and a subendocardial layer containing connective tissue and **Purkinje fibers** (in the ventricles).
- **Myocardium (Middle):**
    - The thickest layer, composed of **cardiac muscle cells (cardiomyocytes)**.
    - Thicker in the ventricles (especially the left) than in the atria.
- **Epicardium (Outer):**
    - Also known as the **visceral layer of the serous pericardium**.
    - Contains blood vessels, nerves, and adipose tissue. Covered by a layer of simple squamous mesothelium.

# 2. Microscopic Characteristics of Cardiomyocytes
Under a light microscope, look for these defining features:
- **Branching:** Unlike skeletal muscle, cardiac cells branch and join together.
- **Nucleus:** Usually a **single, centrally located** oval nucleus.
- **Striations:** Cross-striations are present (due to organized sarcomeres), but less distinct than in skeletal muscle.
- **Intercalated Discs:** Dark-staining transverse lines where two cells meet. These are the most important diagnostic feature.

# 3. Ultramicroscopic Characteristics (Electron Microscopy)
This focuses on the organelles and junctions:
- **Intercalated Discs (The Junctional Complex):**
    - **Fascia Adherens:** Anchors actin filaments; transmits contractile force.
    - **Desmosomes:** Prevent cells from pulling apart during contraction.
    - **Gap Junctions:** Provide electrical coupling, allowing ions to flow between cells for synchronized contraction.
- **Mitochondria:** Extremely numerous (occupying ~40% of cell volume) to support continuous aerobic metabolism.
- **Diads:** The T-tubule and one terminal cisterna of the Sarcoplasmic Reticulum (SR). Note: Skeletal muscle has *triads*; cardiac has *diads* located at the **Z-disc**.
- **Sarcoplasmic Reticulum:** Less developed than in skeletal muscle; requires extracellular Calcium for contraction.

# 4. Functional Characteristics
- **Automaticity:** The heart generates its own electrical impulses (Sinoatrial node).
- **Functional Syncytium:** Because of gap junctions, the myocardium contracts as a single, coordinated unit.
- **Involuntary:** Controlled by the autonomic nervous system, not conscious thought.
- **Refractory Period:** Very long refractory period compared to skeletal muscle, which prevents "tetanus" (cramping), ensuring the heart always relaxes to fill with blood.

# 5. Blood Supply
The heart has a high metabolic demand and requires its own dedicated circulation.
- **Arterial Supply (Coronary Arteries):** Arise from the ascending aorta.
    - **Left Coronary Artery (LCA):** Divides into the Left Anterior Descending (LAD) and Circumflex artery. Supplies the left atrium and most of the left ventricle.
    - **Right Coronary Artery (RCA):** Supplies the right atrium, right ventricle, and the SA/AV nodes (in most people).
- **Venous Drainage:**
    - Most blood drains into the **Coronary Sinus**, which empties directly into the Right Atrium.
    - Major veins: Great, Middle, and Small cardiac veins.

# 6. Nerve Supply
The heart is **innervated by the Autonomic Nervous System (ANS)** via the Cardiac Plexus. Note: The nerves do not *start* the beat; they only *regulate* the rate and force.

- **Parasympathetic Supply:**
    - Source: **Vagus Nerve (CN X)**.
    - Effect: Decreases heart rate (Bradycardia) and decreases force of contraction.
- **Sympathetic Supply:**
    - Source: Cervical and upper thoracic sympathetic ganglia (T1-T4).
    - Effect: Increases heart rate (Tachycardia) and increases force of contraction.
- **Sensory (Afferent):**
    - Travels with sympathetic fibers. This explains **referred pain** during a heart attack (pain felt in the left arm/jaw because they share spinal cord segments).

1. **Can you identify an Intercalated Disc?** (Desmosomes + Gap Junctions).
2. **Where is the nucleus?** (Central).
3. **Diad vs Triad?** (Cardiac = Diad at Z-line).
4. **Main arteries?** (Right and Left Coronaries).
5. **Main nerves?** (Vagus = Slow; Sympathetic = Fast).

# 1. The Heart: General Overview
The heart is a dual-pump system:
- **Right Heart:** Pumps deoxygenated blood to the lungs (**Pulmonary Circulation**).
- **Left Heart:** Pumps oxygenated blood to the body (**Systemic Circulation**).
- **Chambers:** Two superior **atria** (receiving chambers) and two inferior **ventricles** (pumping chambers).

# 2. The Valves of the Heart
The primary function of heart valves is to **ensure unidirectional flow** of blood (prevent backflow). They open and close passively based on **pressure changes**.

### A. Atrioventricular (AV) Valves
Located between the atria and the ventricles.
- **Tricuspid Valve:** Between the Right Atrium and Right Ventricle (3 cusps).
- **Mitral (Bicuspid) Valve:** Between the Left Atrium and Left Ventricle (2 cusps).
- **Support Structures:** 
    - **Chordae Tendineae:** "Heart strings" that tether the valve cusps.
    - **Papillary Muscles:** Contract during ventricular contraction to pull the chordae tendineae taut. This prevents the valves from "everting" (bulging back) into the atria.

![|500](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/-/scassets/images/org/health/articles/21851-tricuspid-valve-illustration)

### B. Semilunar (SL) Valves
Located where blood leaves the ventricles. They are shaped like half-moons and do *not* have chordae tendineae.
- **Pulmonary Valve:** Between Right Ventricle and Pulmonary Artery.
- **Aortic Valve:** Between Left Ventricle and Aorta.

# 3. The Fibrous Skeleton of the Heart
This is a framework of four rings of dense, tough connective tissue (collagen and elastic fibers) surrounding the heart valves.

### Four Main Functions:
1. **Structural Support:** It provides a rigid "foundation" to keep the valve openings from over-stretching.
2. **Point of Attachment:** It serves as the insertion point for the large bundles of cardiac muscle (myocardium).
3. **Valve Anchorage:** It keeps the valves firmly in place.
4. **Electrical Insulation:** **(Crucial for exams)** It acts as a non-conductive barrier between the atria and the ventricles. It prevents the electrical signal from jumping directly from the atria to the ventricles, ensuring the signal only passes through the **AV Bundle**.

# 4. The Conducting System of the Heart
The heart is **autorhythmic**, meaning it generates its own electrical impulses without needing the nervous system.

### The Pathway of the Impulse:
1. **SA Node (Sinoatrial Node):**
    - Located in the wall of the **Right Atrium**.
    - The **"Natural Pacemaker"** of the heart.
    - Generates impulses at a rate of 60–100 beats per minute.
2. **AV Node (Atrioventricular Node):**
    - Located in the interatrial septum.
    - **The "Gatekeeper":** It slows down the signal (AV delay). 
    - *Why?* This delay allows the atria to finish contracting and empty their blood into the ventricles before the ventricles begin to contract.
3. **Bundle of His (Atrioventricular Bundle):**
    - The only electrical connection between the atria and ventricles (passes through the fibrous skeleton).
4. **Right and Left Bundle Branches:**
    - The signal travels down the interventricular septum toward the apex (bottom) of the heart.
5. **Purkinje Fibers:**
    - Large fibers that spread the signal rapidly through the ventricular walls.
    - Ensures that the ventricles contract from the **apex upward**, squeezing blood out toward the great arteries.

# 5. Functional Correlation: The Heart Sounds
- **S1 ("Lubb"):** Caused by the closure of the **AV valves** at the start of ventricular contraction (systole).
- **S2 ("Dupp"):** Caused by the closure of the **Semilunar valves** at the start of ventricular relaxation (diastole).

---

# Summary for Quick Revision
1. **Why do ventricles contract from the bottom up?** Because the Purkinje fibers start at the apex. This is more efficient for pushing blood out through the superiorly located arteries.
2. **What happens if the SA node fails?** The AV node takes over, but at a slower rate (40–60 bpm).
3. **The Role of the Fibrous Skeleton in Rhythm:** Without it, the whole heart would contract at once, and the ventricles wouldn't have time to fill with blood.
4. **Valves are PASSIVE:** They do not use energy to open or close; they simply respond to the pressure of blood pushing against them.

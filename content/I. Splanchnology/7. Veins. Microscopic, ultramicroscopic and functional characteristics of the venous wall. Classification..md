> Veins. Microscopic, ultramicroscopic and functional characteristics of the venous wall. Classification.


---

# 1. General Functional Characteristics
1. **Capacitance Vessels:**
	- Veins are highly distensible
	- Act as the primary blood reservoir, holding roughly **60–70% of total blood volume**.
2. **Low Pressure System:**
	- Blood pressure is significantly lower than in arteries
	- Approx. 10 mmHg to 0 mmHg near the heart
3. **Unidirectional Flow:**
	- Facilitated by **valves** (semilunar folds of the tunica intima)
	- Prevent backflow, especially in the lower extremities.
4. **Response to Stimuli:**
	- Veins constrict (venoconstriction) via sympathetic stimulation
	- Increase venous return during exercise or hemorrhage.

# 2. Microscopic Structure of the Venous Wall
Like arteries, veins have three layers (tunics), but the boundaries between them are often blurred.

![|500](https://s3-us-west-2.amazonaws.com/courses-images-archive-read-only/wp-content/uploads/sites/403/2015/04/21031418/2102_Comparison_of_Artery_and_Vein.jpg)

## A. Tunica Intima
- **Endothelium:** Simple squamous epithelium.
- **Subendothelial layer:** Very thin layer of connective tissue.
- **Internal Elastic Lamina:** Usually absent or very poorly developed.
- **Valves:** Characteristic of medium-sized veins. They consist of a core of connective tissue covered by endothelium.

## B. Tunica Media
- **Composition:** Much thinner than in corresponding arteries.
- **Structure:** Consists of several layers of circularly arranged smooth muscle cells intermixed with collagen and elastic fibers.
- **Note:** In large veins, this layer is surprisingly thin.

## C. Tunica Adventitia
- **Composition:** Usually the **thickest layer** of the venous wall.
- **Structure:** Composed of longitudinal collagen and elastic fibers.
- **Vasa Vasorum:** More abundant in veins than arteries because venous blood is nutrient-poor and low in oxygen; the wall needs external nourishment.
- **Large Veins Specificity:** Contains bundles of **longitudinally arranged smooth muscle**.

---

# 3. Ultramicroscopic Characteristics (Electron Microscopy)

- **Endothelial Cells:** Contain **Weibel-Palade bodies** (storing von Willebrand factor and P-selectin).
- **Junctions:** Possess tight junctions (zonula occludens) and gap junctions, but in post-capillary venules, these junctions are "loose," allowing for leukocyte diapedesis (migration).
- **Pericytes:** Found wrapped around post-capillary venules; they have contractile properties and can differentiate into smooth muscle or fibroblasts during repair.
- **Basal Lamina:** Continuous in most veins.

# 4. Classification of Veins

![|500](https://cdn.lecturio.com/assets/Comparison-of-vein-types.jpg)

## I. Venules
1. **Post-capillary Venules (0.1–0.5 mm):**
    - Structure: Endothelium + Pericytes.
    - Function: Primary site of **leukocyte emigration** (diapedesis) and inflammation response (sensitive to histamine).
2. **Muscular Venules:**
    - Structure: Gain 1–2 layers of smooth muscle in the tunica media.
## II. Small and Medium Veins (1–10 mm)
- **Tunica Intima:** Thin; possesses **valves**.
- **Tunica Media:** Thin circular smooth muscle.
- **Tunica Adventitia:** Well-developed collagenous layer.
- Examples: Brachial, Radial, Tibial Veins.
## III. Large Veins
- **Tunica Intima:** Relatively thick compared to smaller veins.
- **Tunica Media:** Very thin.
- **Tunica Adventitia:** Extremely thick. **Unique feature:** Contains "myocardial sleeves" or **longitudinal bundles of smooth muscle**. This allows the vein to shorten and lengthen with breathing and prevents distension under gravity
- Examples: Superior and Inferior Vena Cava, Portal Vein.

# 5. Summary for Quick Revision

| Feature | Artery | Vein |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Lumen** | Narrow, circular | Wide, often collapsed/irregular |
| **Wall Thickness** | Thick | Thin |
| **Thickest Layer** | Tunica Media | Tunica Adventitia |
| **Valves** | Absent | Present (in many) |
| **Internal Elastic Lamina**| Prominent | Absent/Weak |

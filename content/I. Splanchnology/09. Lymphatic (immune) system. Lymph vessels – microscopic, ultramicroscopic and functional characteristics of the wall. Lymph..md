# 1. Overview of the Lymphatic System
The lymphatic system is a specialized component of the circulatory and immune systems. It consists of lymph fluid, lymphatic vessels, and lymphoid organs.
- **Primary function:** Maintenance of fluid balance, absorption of dietary fats (lacteals), and defense against pathogens (immune response).

---

# 2. Lymph (The Fluid)
Lymph is a clear, straw-colored ultrafiltrate of blood plasma.
- **Formation:** Formed when interstitial fluid enters the lymphatic capillaries.
- **Composition:**
    - **Water and Electrolytes**
    - **Proteins**
    - **Lipids:** High concentration in "chyle" (lymph from the small intestine).
    - **Cells:** Predominantly **lymphocytes**. Under normal conditions, there are **no erythrocytes (RBCs)** and very few granulocytes.
    - **Coagulation factors**

# 3. Lymphatic Capillaries (Microscopic & Ultramicroscopic)
Lymphatic capillaries are tiny **blind-ended tubes** that collect excess fluid from tissues.

- **Structure**
	- They are **wider and more irregular** than blood capillaries.
	- Their walls are made of a **single thin layer of endothelial cells**.
	- They have a very **leaky wall**, making it easy for fluid and large particles to enter.
- **Special Features**
	- The endothelial cells **overlap like roof shingles**, forming **one-way valves**.
	- **Anchoring filaments** connect the capillary wall to the surrounding tissue.

# 4. Microscopic
As capillaries merge, they form larger vessels that resemble veins but have thinner walls.

- **Wall Structure (Three Tunics):**
    1. **Tunica Intima:** Endothelium and a thin subendothelial layer. Contains **valves**.
    2. **Tunica Media:** Several layers of **smooth muscle cells** arranged helically and elastic fibers.
    3. **Tunica Adventitia:** Outer layer of connective tissue (collagen and elastic fibers) that blends with surrounding tissue.
- **The Lymphangion:** 
    - The functional unit of a lymph vessel, defined as the segment **between two valves**. 
    - It contracts rhythmically (intrinsic pump) to push lymph forward.

# 5. Large Lymphatic Ducts (Thoracic Duct & Right Lymphatic Duct)
These are the largest vessels that empty into the subclavian veins.

- **Wall Characteristics:**
    - The wall is more distinct but the boundaries between the three tunics are still blurry.
    - The **tunica media** is the thickest layer, containing significant smooth muscle.
    - Contains **Vasa Vasorum** (small blood vessels) and nerves in the adventitia to supply the thick wall.

# 6. Functional Characteristics of the Vessel Walls
1. **Highly Permeable**
    - Lymphatic vessels are much more "leaky" than blood vessels.
    - They can take up large proteins, waste particles, bacteria, and even cells such as lymphocytes or cancer cells.
2. **Many Valves**
    - Lymphatic vessels contain numerous valves.
    - These valves prevent backflow and ensure that lymph moves in **one direction only—toward the heart**.
3. **Help Move Lymph**
4. **Immune Surveillance**
    - Lymphatic vessels pass through **lymph nodes**.
    - Here, the lymph is filtered and checked for pathogens, foreign particles, and abnormal cells before returning to the bloodstream.

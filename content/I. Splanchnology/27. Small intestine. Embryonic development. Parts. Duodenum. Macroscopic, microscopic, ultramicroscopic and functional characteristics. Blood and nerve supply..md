> Small intestine. Embryonic development. Parts. Duodenum. Macroscopic, microscopic, ultramicroscopic and functional characteristics. Blood and nerve supply.

---

# 1. Embryonic Development
- **Origin:** Derived from both **Foregut** and **Midgut**.
    - **Foregut:** Forms the duodenum proximal to the entry of the bile duct.
    - **Midgut:** Forms the duodenum distal to the bile duct, the jejunum, and the ileum.
- **Key Process:** 
    - The lumen is initially hollow, then fills with solid cells (proliferation).
    -  **Recanalization:** The solid lumen clears to become a tube again. 

---

# 2. General Parts
1. **Duodenum:** C-shaped, shortest (25 cm), mostly retroperitoneal.
2. **Jejunum:** Proximal 2/5ths, wider lumen, thicker wall, long vasa recta.
3. **Ileum:** Distal 3/5ths, thinner wall, many Peyer’s patches (lymphoid tissue).

# 3. The Duodenum (Focus)
## Macroscopic (Gross Anatomy)
- **Shape:** "C" shape surrounding the head of the pancreas.
- **4 Parts:**
    1. **Superior (1st):** L1 level. Only part that is intraperitoneal. Common site for ulcers.
    2. **Descending (2nd):** L2 level. Contains the **Major Duodenal Papilla**.
    3. **Horizontal (3rd):** L3 level. Crosses anterior to the IVC and Aorta.
    4. **Ascending (4th):** L4 level. Joins jejunum at the Duodenojejunal flexure.

![|500](https://teachmeanatomy.info/wp-content/uploads/Parts-of-the-Duodenum-Small-Intestine.jpg)
## Microscopic (Histology)
- **Mucosa:** 
    - Villi (finger-like projections).
    - Crypts of Lieberkühn (simple tubular glands).
- **Submucosa:** **Brunner’s Glands**. These secrete alkaline mucus to neutralize stomach acid.
- **Muscularis Externa:** Inner circular and outer longitudinal smooth muscle layers.
- **Serosa/Adventitia:** Mostly adventitia (because it's retroperitoneal).

## Ultramicroscopic (Cellular Level)
- **Enterocytes:** Columnar cells with **Microvilli** (forming the "Brush Border") for absorption.
- **Goblet Cells:** Secrete mucus.
- **Paneth Cells:** Located in crypt bases; secrete lysozymes (anti-bacterial).
- **Enteroendocrine Cells:** Secrete hormones:
    - **S-cells:** Secretin (stimulates bicarb).
    - **I-cells:** Cholecystokinin/CCK (stimulates bile/enzymes).

## Functional Characteristics
1. **Neutralization:** Brunner’s glands + Pancreatic bicarbonate raise pH.
2. **Digestion:** Primary site for chemical digestion.
3. **Absorption:** Specifically iron and calcium.

---

# 4. Blood Supply
- **Arterial:** Transition point between Foregut and Midgut.
    - **Proximal (above papilla):** Superior Pancreaticoduodenal A.
    - **Distal (below papilla):** Inferior Pancreaticoduodenal A.
- **Venous:** Drains into the **Portal Vein**.

---

# 5. Nerve Supply
- **Autonomic:**
    - **Parasympathetic:** Vagus Nerve (increases motility and secretion).
    - **Sympathetic:** Celiac and Superior Mesenteric Plexus (decreases motility/vasoconstriction).
- **Enteric Nervous System (Intrinsic):**
    - **Meissner’s Plexus:** In the submucosa (controls secretions).
    - **Auerbach’s (Myenteric) Plexus:** Between muscle layers (controls peristalsis).

---

# Quick Summary for Revision
- **Brunner's Glands** = Duodenum.
- **Peyer's Patches** = Ileum.
- **Double Bubble** = Duodenal Atresia.
- **Major Papilla** = 2nd part of duodenum.
- **Recanalization** = Key embryonic step.

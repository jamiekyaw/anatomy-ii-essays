> Small intestine. Jejunum and ileum. Macroscopic, microscopic, ultramicroscopic and functional characteristics. Blood and nerve supply.

---

# 1. Macroscopic Characteristics (Gross Anatomy)
The small intestine is ~6 meters long. The jejunum (proximal 2/5) and ileum (distal 3/5) are intraperitoneal and suspended by the **mesentery**.

| Feature               | Jejunum (Upper Left Quadrant) | Ileum (Lower Right Quadrant)           |
| :-------------------- | :---------------------------- | :------------------------------------- |
| **Wall**              | Thick and heavy               | Thin and light                         |
| **Vascularity**       | High                          | Low                                    |
| **Vasa Recta**        | Long                          | Short                                  |
| **Arcades**           | Few, large loops              | Many, short loops                      |
| **Fat in Mesentery**  | Less                          | More                                   |
| **Plicae Circulares** | Large, tall, and closely set  | Low and sparse (absent in distal part) |
| **Lymphoid Tissue**   | Sparse                        | **Peyer’s Patches**                    |

# 2. Microscopic Characteristics (Histology)
The wall consists of 4 standard GI layers:
1. **Mucosa:** 
    - **Villi:** Finger-like projections to increase surface area.
    - **Crypts of Lieberkühn:** Deep glands between villi.
2. **Submucosa:** Contains Meissner’s nerve plexus. 
3. **Muscularis Externa:** Inner circular and outer longitudinal smooth muscle. Contains Auerbach’s plexus.
4. **Serosa:** Outer visceral peritoneum.

# 3. Ultramicroscopic Characteristics (Cellular level)
- **Enterocytes:** Absorptive cells with **Microvilli**.
- Function: Increase surface area.
- **Goblet Cells:** Secrete mucus for lubrication.
- **Paneth Cells:** Found in crypt bases; secrete **lysozyme** (antibacterial).
- **Enteroendocrine Cells:** Secrete hormones (CCK, Secretin, GIP).
- **M-Cells (Microfold cells):** Sample antigens from the gut lumen.

---

# 4. Functional Characteristics
- **Digestion:**
	- Final chemical breakdown of carbohydrates, proteins, and lipids via brush-border enzymes (maltase, lactase, peptidases).
- **Absorption:** 
    - **Jejunum:** Primary site for most nutrients (glucose, amino acids, vitamins).
    - **Ileum:** Specific absorption of **Vitamin B12** (via Intrinsic Factor) and **Bile Salts**.
- **Motility:** Peristalsis and Segmentation (mixing).
- **Immune:** Peyer’s Patches.

---

# 5. Blood Supply
- **Arterial:** **Superior Mesenteric Artery (SMA)**. 
    - Branches into Jejunal and Ileal arteries.
- **Venous:** **Superior Mesenteric Vein (SMV)**.
    - Joins the Splenic vein to form the **Hepatic Portal Vein.

---

# 6. Nerve Supply
- **Extrinsic (Autonomic):**
    - **Parasympathetic:** Vagus Nerve (CN X) — *Stimulates* motility and secretion.
    - **Sympathetic:** Splanchnic nerves (T5-T9) — *Inhibits* motility, causes vasoconstriction.
- **Intrinsic (Enteric Nervous System):**
    - **Myenteric (Auerbach’s) Plexus:** Controls muscle contraction (motility).
    - **Submucosal (Meissner’s) Plexus:** Controls secretions and local blood flow.

---

# Summary for Quick Revision
1. **Jejunum** = Thick wall, long vasa recta, high plicae, main absorption site.
2. **Ileum** = Thin wall, Peyer’s patches, B12/Bile salt absorption.
3. **SMA** is the main artery; **Vagus** is the main stimulatory nerve.
4. **Microvilli** = Brush border = Surface area for absorption.

> Pharynx - Macroscopic, microscopic, ultramicroscopic and functional characteristics. Blood and nerve supply.

---

# 1. Macroscopic Anatomy (Gross Structure)
The pharynx is a 12–14 cm muscular tube extending from the **base of the skull** to the **lower border of the cricoid cartilage (C6)**.

**Three Divisions:**
1. **Nasopharynx (Respiratory):** Behind the nasal cavity. 
    - *Key features:* Pharyngeal tonsils, Opening of Eustachian tube.
2. **Oropharynx (Digestive/Respiratory):** Behind the oral cavity. 
    - *Key features:* Palatine tonsils.
3. **Laryngopharynx (Digestive):** From epiglottis to esophagus.
    - *Key features:* Piriform fossae (food channels).

**Muscles:**
- **External Circular (Constrictors):** Superior, Middle, and Inferior. They constrict the pharynx to push food down.
- **Internal Longitudinal (Elevators):** Stylopharyngeus, Salpingopharyngeus, Palatopharyngeus. They lift the pharynx during swallowing.

# 2. Microscopic Anatomy (Histology)
The wall consists of four layers (inner to outer):

1. **Mucosa:**
    - **Nasopharynx:** Pseudostratified columnar ciliated epithelium (Respiratory type).
    - **Oro/Laryngopharynx:** Non-keratinized stratified squamous epithelium.
2. **Submucosa:** Contains the **Pharyngobasilar fascia** (anchors the pharynx to the skull).
3. **Muscularis:** Skeletal muscle (voluntary control for swallowing).
4. **Adventitia:** **Buccopharyngeal fascia** (allows movement against the vertebral column).

---

# 3. Ultramicroscopic (Cellular/EM Level)
- **Ciliated Cells (Nasopharynx):** High density of mitochondria to power cilia; 9+2 microtubule arrangement.
- **Goblet Cells:** Membrane-bound mucinogen granules for mucus secretion.
- **Squamous Cells:** Numerous **desmosomes** (cell-to-cell junctions) to provide mechanical strength against bolus abrasion.
- **M-Cells:** Found over lymphoid tissue (tonsils) to sample antigens.

---

# 4. Functional Characteristics
1. **Deglutition (Swallowing):** Coordination of muscles to move bolus from mouth to esophagus.
2. **Respiration:** Provides a passage for air.
3. **Protection:** **Waldeyer’s Ring** (tonsils) provides a lymphoid barrier against pathogens.
4. **Hearing:** The Eustachian tube in the nasopharynx equalizes pressure in the middle ear.
5. **Resonance:** Acts as a chamber for speech sounds.

---

# 5. Blood Supply
- **Arterial (Mainly External Carotid branches):**
    - Ascending pharyngeal artery (Primary).
    - Branches of Facial, Maxillary, and Lingual arteries.
- **Venous Drainage:**
    - Pharyngeal venous plexus $\rightarrow$ **Internal Jugular Vein**.

---

# 6. Nerve Supply (The Pharyngeal Plexus)
Located on the middle constrictor muscle.

- **Motor Supply:** 
    - **Vagus Nerve (CN X):** Supplies ALL pharyngeal muscles EXCEPT the Stylopharyngeus.
    - **Glossopharyngeal Nerve (CN IX):** Supplies the **Stylopharyngeus** muscle only.
- **Sensory Supply:**
    - **Nasopharynx:** Maxillary Nerve (V2).
    - **Oropharynx:** Glossopharyngeal Nerve (CN IX).
    - **Laryngopharynx:** Vagus Nerve (CN X - Internal laryngeal branch).

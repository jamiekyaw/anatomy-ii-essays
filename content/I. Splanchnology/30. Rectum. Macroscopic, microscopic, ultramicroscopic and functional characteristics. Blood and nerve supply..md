> Rectum. Macroscopic, microscopic, ultramicroscopic and functional characteristics. Blood and nerve supply.

---

# 1. Macroscopic Characteristics (Gross Anatomy)
- **Location:** Extends from **S3 vertebra** to the **anal canal**.
- **Length:** Approximately 12–15 cm.
- **Distinguishing Features (What it LACKS compared to colon):** 
    - No Taeniae coli.
    - No Haustra.
    - No Appendices epiploicae.
- **Flexures:**
    - **Sacral flexure:** Follows the curve of the sacrum.
    - **Perineal flexure**.
- **Internal Folds:** **Valves of Houston**.
- **Rectal Ampulla:** The dilated lower part that stores feces before defecation.

# 2. Microscopic Characteristics (Histology)
- **Mucosa:** 
    - **Epithelium:** Simple columnar with a high concentration of **Goblet cells**.
    - **Crypts of Lieberkühn:** Deep, straight, tubular glands.
    - **Lamina Propria:** Rich in lymphoid tissue (MALT).
- **Submucosa:** Contains dense connective tissue, blood vessels, and **Meissner’s plexus**.
- **Muscularis Externa:** 
    - Inner circular layer.
    - Outer longitudinal layer.
    - **Auerbach’s (Myenteric) plexus** lies between them.
- **Serosa/Adventitia:** Upper 1/3 is serosa; middle 1/3 is serosa; lower 1/3 is adventitia.

# 3. Ultramicroscopic (EM) Characteristics
- **Enterocytes:** Feature short, irregular **microvilli** for water/electrolyte absorption.
- **Goblet Cells:** Packed with **mucinogen granules** in the apical portion.
- **Enteroendocrine Cells:** Contain small basal granules for hormone release (e.g., serotonin).
- **Stem Cells:** Located at the base of the crypts for rapid epithelial turnover (every 3–5 days).

# 4. Functional Characteristics
- **Storage:** The ampulla holds fecal matter.
- **Absorption:** Absorbs water, electrolytes, and certain drugs.
- **Defecation Reflex:** Stretch receptors in the rectal wall detect distension and signal the brain/spinal cord to initiate the urge to defecate.

# 5. Blood Supply
- **Arterial:**
    1. **Superior Rectal Artery**.
    2. **Middle Rectal Artery**.
    3. **Inferior Rectal Artery**.
- **Venous Drainage:**
    - **Superior Rectal Vein** $\rightarrow$ Inferior Mesenteric Vein $\rightarrow$ **Portal System**.
    - **Middle/Inferior Rectal Veins** $\rightarrow$ Internal Iliac Veins $\rightarrow$ **Systemic System (IVC)**.

# 6. Nerve Supply
- **Sympathetic:** Lumbar splanchnic nerves (L1-L2) via the hypogastric plexuses. 
    - *Function:* Inhibits rectal contraction; maintains internal sphincter tone.
- **Parasympathetic:** Pelvic splanchnic nerves (**S2-S4**). 
    - *Function:* Stimulates rectal contraction; relaxes internal sphincter (Defecation).
- **Sensory:** Stretch fibers travel with parasympathetic nerves to S2-S4 (detects fullness).

# Summary for Quick Revision
1. **Starts at?** S3.
2. **Missing features?** No taeniae, no haustra.
3. **Epithelium?** Simple columnar + Goblet cells.
4. **Main Artery?** Superior Rectal (from IMA).
5. **Parasympathetic?** S2, 3, 4 (keeps the poop off the floor).

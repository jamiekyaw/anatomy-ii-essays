> Cardiovascular system. General microscopic, ultramicroscopic and functional characteristics of its organs. Major (or systematic) circulation and lesser (or pulmonary) circulation. Embryonic development of major blood vessels. Fetal circulation.

---

# 1. General Microscopic & Ultramicroscopic Structure
The CVS consists of the **heart** and **blood vessels**. Most blood vessels share a common 3-layered pattern.

## A. The Heart Wall
1. **Endocardium:** Inner layer. Endothelium + subendothelial connective tissue + **Purkinje fibers** (in the subendocardial layer).
2. **Myocardium:** Thickest layer. Composed of cardiac muscle cells (cardiomyocytes).
3. **Epicardium:** Outer layer (visceral pericardium). Contains coronary vessels, nerves, and fat.

![](https://vmicro.iusm.iu.edu/hs_vm/images/heart_diagram_image.jpg)

## B. The Blood Vessel Wall
- **Tunica Intima:** Endothelial cells (simple squamous) sitting on a basement membrane. Provides a non-thrombogenic surface.
- **Tunica Media:** Smooth muscle cells and elastic fibers. Thickest in arteries. Responsible for vasoconstriction/dilation.
- **Tunica Adventitia:** Connective tissue (collagen/elastic fibers). Contains **Vasa Vasorum** (vessels of the vessels) in large arteries/veins.

![|500](https://s3-us-west-2.amazonaws.com/courses-images-archive-read-only/wp-content/uploads/sites/403/2015/04/21031418/2102_Comparison_of_Artery_and_Vein.jpg)

## C. Ultramicroscopic Characteristics (Fine Detail)
- **Endothelial Cells:** Contain **Weibel-Palade bodies** (storing von Willebrand factor for clotting). They have tight junctions and pinocytotic vesicles for transport.
- **Cardiomyocytes:** Feature **Intercalated Discs**. These contain:
    - *Fascia adherens/Desmosomes:* Mechanical coupling (hold cells together).
    - *Gap junctions:* Electrical coupling (allows ions to flow, so heart contracts as a syncytium).
- **Capillaries:** Only consist of endothelium and a basal lamina.
    - *Continuous:* Muscle, lungs (tight).
    - *Fenestrated:* Kidney, endocrine glands (pores for filtration).
    - *Sinusoidal:* Liver, spleen, bone marrow (large gaps for cell passage).

# 2. Major vs. Lesser Circulation

## A. Lesser (Pulmonary) Circulation
- **Path:** Right Ventricle $\rightarrow$ Pulmonary Trunk $\rightarrow$ Pulmonary Arteries $\rightarrow$ Lung Capillaries $\rightarrow$ Pulmonary Veins $\rightarrow$ Left Atrium.
- **Function:** Gas exchange (re-oxygenation of blood and removal of $CO_2$).
- **Pressure:** Low pressure system.

## B. Major (Systemic) Circulation
- **Path:** Left Ventricle $\rightarrow$ Aorta $\rightarrow$ Systemic Arteries $\rightarrow$ Tissue Capillaries $\rightarrow$ Systemic Veins $\rightarrow$ Venae Cavae $\rightarrow$ Right Atrium.
- **Function:** Delivery of $O_2$, nutrients, and hormones to all tissues; collection of metabolic waste.
- **Pressure:** High pressure system.

# 3. Embryonic Development of Major Blood Vessels
Vessels develop from **Mesoderm** via two processes: *Vasculogenesis* (formation of new vessels from angioblasts) and *Angiogenesis* (sprouting from existing vessels).

## A. Arterial System (Aortic Arches)
The pharyngeal arches each receive an artery. Key derivatives:
- **3rd Arch:** Common Carotid and proximal Internal Carotid arteries.
- **4th Arch:** On the left = **Arch of the Aorta**; on the right = Right Subclavian artery.
- **6th Arch:** Pulmonary arteries. On the left, it forms the **Ductus Arteriosus**.

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/8/84/Gray473.png)

## B. Venous System
Three major pairs of veins in the embryo:
1. **Vitelline Veins:** Carry blood from the yolk sac; form the liver sinusoids and the portal system.
2. **Umbilical Veins:** Carry oxygenated blood from the placenta.
3. **Cardinal Veins:** Form the main drainage of the embryo body (SVC and IVC).

# 4. Fetal Circulation
The fetus does not use its lungs or GI tract for gas/nutrient exchange; it uses the **Placenta**. There are **three shunts** to bypass the lungs and liver.

## A. The Path of Blood
1. Oxygenated blood from placenta $\rightarrow$ **Umbilical Vein**.
2. **Shunt 1 (Ductus Venosus):** Bypasses the liver, dumping blood directly into the Inferior Vena Cava (IVC).
3. Blood enters the Right Atrium.
4. **Shunt 2 (Foramen Ovale):** An opening between the right and left atria. Most blood flows Right $\rightarrow$ Left Atrium to bypass the lungs, then to the Left Ventricle and Aorta.
5. Blood that enters the Right Ventricle goes to the Pulmonary Trunk.
6. **Shunt 3 (Ductus Arteriosus):** A vessel connecting the Pulmonary Trunk to the Aorta. It bypasses the fluid-filled lungs.
7. Blood returns to the placenta via **two Umbilical Arteries**.

## B. Changes at Birth
When the baby takes its first breath, lung resistance drops and pressure changes cause the shunts to close:
- **Umbilical Vein** $\rightarrow$ *Ligamentum teres hepatis*.
- **Ductus Venosus** $\rightarrow$ *Ligamentum venosum*.
- **Foramen Ovale** $\rightarrow$ *Fossa ovalis* (closed by pressure).
- **Ductus Arteriosus** $\rightarrow$ *Ligamentum arteriosum*.
- **Umbilical Arteries** $\rightarrow$ *Medial umbilical ligaments*.

# Summary Table for Quick Revision

| Feature | Artery | Vein |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Wall Thickness** | Thick (tunica media dominates) | Thin (tunica adventitia dominates) |
| **Lumen** | Narrow, circular | Wide, often collapsed |
| **Valves** | Absent | Present (especially in limbs) |
| **Pressure** | High | Low |
| **Function** | Resistance/Distribution | Capacitance (Blood reservoir) |

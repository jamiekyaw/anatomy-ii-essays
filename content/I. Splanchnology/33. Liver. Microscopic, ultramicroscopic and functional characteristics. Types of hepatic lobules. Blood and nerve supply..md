> Liver. Microscopic, ultramicroscopic and functional characteristics. Types of hepatic lobules. Blood and nerve supply.

---

# 1. Microscopic Structure
- **Stroma:**
	- **Glisson’s capsule** is a connective tissue that branches into the liver to divide it into lobules.
- **Parenchyma:**
	- Made of **Hepatocytes**
	- Arranged in "cords" 1-2 cells thick.
-   **Sinusoids:**
	- Large, leaky capillaries between hepatocyte cords. 
    - **Kupffer Cells:** Fixed macrophages inside sinusoids (eat bacteria/old RBCs).
-   **Space of Disse:**
	- The narrow gap between hepatocytes and sinusoids.
	- This is where nutrient exchange happens.
-   **Biliary Canaliculi:**
	- Tiny channels between hepatocytes that carry bile.

# 2. Ultramicroscopic
-   **Abundant Mitochondria:** For high energy needs.
-   **Smooth ER:** For **detoxification** of drugs/alcohol and lipid metabolism.
-   **Rough ER:** For synthesis of **plasma proteins** (Albumin, Clotting factors).
-   **Peroxisomes:** Break down hydrogen peroxide and fatty acids.
-   **Glycosomes:** Granules for glycogen storage.

# 3. Functional Characteristics
- **Exocrine:** Produces **Bile** (stored in gallbladder).
- **Endocrine:** Releases **Albumin, Fibrinogen, and Glucose** directly into the blood.
- **Metabolic:** Storage of glycogen, Vitamins (A, D, K, B12), and Iron.
- **Detoxification:** Converts ammonia to urea; breaks down toxins.

# 4. Types of Hepatic Lobules 
1.  **Classical Lobule (Hexagonal):**
    - **Center:** Central Vein.
    - **Periphery:** Portal Triads.
    - **Function:** Explains **blood flow** from periphery to center.
2.  **Portal Lobule (Triangular):**
    - **Center:** Portal Triad.
    - **Points:** Three Central Veins.
    - **Function:** Explains **bile secretion** flows toward the center triad.
3.  **Liver Acinus (Diamond-shaped):**
    - **Location:** Between two central veins and two portal triads.
    - **Function:** Explains **Oxygenation/Metabolism**.
    - Divided into 3 Zones:
        - *Zone 1:- Closest to blood supply
	        - (best oxygen, first to regenerate).
        - *Zone 3:- Closest to central vein
	        - (poorest oxygen, first to die in ischemia).

# 5. Blood and Nerve Supply
The liver receives blood from two sources:
- **Hepatic Artery (20-25%):** Oxygenated blood.
- **Portal Vein (75-80%):** Deoxygenated but **nutrient-rich** blood from the GI tract.
- **Venous Drainage:** Sinusoids → Central Veins → Hepatic Veins → Inferior Vena Cava.

**Nerve Supply:**
-   **Sympathetic:**
	- From the Celiac Plexus (vasoconstriction).
-   **Parasympathetic:**
	- From the Vagus Nerve (weak effect, mostly gallbladder contraction).

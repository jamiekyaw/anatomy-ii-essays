> Esophagus. Embryonic development. Macroscopic, microscopic, ultramicroscopic and functional characteristics. Blood and nerve supply.

---

# 1. Embryonic Development
- **Origin:** Derived from the **foregut** (endoderm).
- **Partitioning:** The **tracheoesophageal septum** separates the respiratory primordium (ventral) from the esophagus (dorsal).
- **Muscle Origin:** 
    - Upper (skeletal): Mesoderm of pharyngeal arches 4 and 6.
    - Lower (smooth): Splanchnic mesoderm.

# 2. Macroscopic Characteristics
- **Length:** ~25 cm long muscular tube.
- **Extent:** Starts at **C6** (cricoid cartilage) and ends at **T11** (cardiac orifice of stomach).
- **Three Parts:** Cervical, Thoracic, and Abdominal.
- **Anatomic Constrictions (The "ABCDs"):**
    1. **A**rch of Aorta
    2. **B**ronchus
    3. **C**ricopharyngeus muscle
    4. **D**iaphragmatic hiatus

# 3. Microscopic Characteristics (Histology)
The esophagus follows the general GI plan but with specific tweaks:
- **Mucosa:**
    - **Epithelium:** Non-keratinized **stratified squamous**.
    - **Lamina Propria:** Cardiac glands.
    - **Muscularis Mucosae:** Unusually thick, longitudinal smooth muscle.
- **Submucosa:** Contains **Esophageal Glands Proper** (mucus-secreting) and Meissner’s plexus.
- **Muscularis Propria:**
    - Upper 1/3: **Skeletal muscle** (voluntary).
    - Middle 1/3: **Mixed** (skeletal and smooth).
    - Lower 1/3: **Smooth muscle** (involuntary).
    - Auerbach’s (Myenteric) plexus lies between the inner circular and outer longitudinal layers.
- **Adventitia/Serosa:** Mostly **Adventitia** (connective tissue). Only the short abdominal portion is covered by **Serosa** (peritoneum).

# 4. Ultramicroscopic Characteristics (EM level)
- **Epithelium:** Cells are joined by numerous **desmosomes** to resist mechanical stress; cytoplasm contains **tonofilaments**.
- **Glands:** Submucosal cells show abundant Rough ER and Golgi apparatus.
- **Nerve endings:** Synaptic vesicles containing neurotransmitters.

# 5. Functional Characteristics
- **Transport:** Moves food bolus via **peristalsis**.
- **Protection:** Stratified squamous epithelium protects against rough food; mucus glands lubricate and neutralize refluxed acid.
- **Sphincter Action:**
    - **Upper Esophageal Sphincter (UES):** Prevents air from entering during respiration.
    - **Lower Esophageal Sphincter (LES):** Physiological sphincter that prevents gastric acid reflux.

# 6. Blood Supply
- **Arterial:**
    - Upper: Inferior thyroid arteries.
    - Middle: Esophageal branches of the Thoracic Aorta.
    - Lower: Left gastric artery (branch of Celiac trunk) and Left inferior phrenic artery.
- **Venous:**
    - Upper/Middle: Drain into **Azygos/Hemiazygos** (Systemic circulation).
    - Lower: Drains into **Left Gastric Vein** (Portal circulation).

# 7. Nerve Supply
- **Parasympathetic (Motor/Secretory):** **Vagus Nerve (CN X)**. Increases peristalsis and gland secretion.
- **Sympathetic (Inhibitory):** Sympathetic trunks (T1-T4). Decreases peristalsis, causes vasoconstriction.
- **Enteric Nervous System:**
    - **Meissner’s Plexus:** Controls secretions (in submucosa).
    - **Auerbach’s Plexus:** Controls motility (between muscle layers).

---

# Summary for Quick Revision
- **Epithelium:** Stratified squamous.
- **Muscle transition:** Skeletal $\rightarrow$ Mixed $\rightarrow$ Smooth.
- **Venous:** Portal-Systemic shunt (Left Gastric/Azygos).
- **Development:** Foregut + Tracheoesophageal septum.

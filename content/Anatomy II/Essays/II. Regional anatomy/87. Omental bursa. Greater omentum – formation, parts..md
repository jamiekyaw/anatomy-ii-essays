# 1. Omental Bursa (Lesser Sac)
- **Definition:** A large fluid-filled space located **behind** the stomach and liver.
- **Purpose:** Allows the stomach to move freely without friction against other organs.
- **Boundaries (The basics):**
    - **Anterior (Front):** Stomach and Lesser Omentum.
    - **Posterior (Back):** Pancreas and Left Kidney.
- **Communication:** It connects to the rest of the abdominal cavity (Greater Sac) via the **Omental Foramen** (also called the *Foramen of Winslow*).

# 2. Greater Omentum
- **Definition:** A large, "apron-like" fold of peritoneum that hangs down from the stomach.
- **Formation:**
    - It is formed by **4 layers** of peritoneum (which usually fuse together in adults).
    - It starts at the **Greater Curvature** of the stomach.
    - It drapes down over the small intestines and folds back up to attach to the **Transverse Colon**.
- **Nickname:** Known as the **"Policeman of the Abdomen"** because it migrates to sites of infection or inflammation to "wrap" them and prevent the spread of peritonitis.

# 3. Parts (Ligaments) of the Greater Omentum
The Greater Omentum is technically composed of three continuous ligaments:
1. **Gastrocolic Ligament:** The largest part connecting the stomach to the transverse colon.
2. **Gastrosplenic Ligament:** Connects the stomach to the spleen.
3. **Gastrophrenic Ligament:** Connects the stomach to the diaphragm.

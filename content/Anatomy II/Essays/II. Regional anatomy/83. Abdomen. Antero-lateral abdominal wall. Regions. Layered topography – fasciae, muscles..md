> Abdomen. Antero-lateral abdominal wall. Regions. Layered topography – fasciae, muscles.

---

# 1. Regions of the Abdomen
The abdomen is divided into 9 regions by two vertical lines (mid-clavicular) and two horizontal lines (subcostal and intertubercular).

| | **Right Side** | **Middle (Midline)** | **Left Side** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Top Row** | Right Hypochondrium | Epigastric | Left Hypochondrium |
| **Middle Row** | Right Lumbar (Flank) | Umbilical | Left Lumbar (Flank) |
| **Bottom Row** | Right Iliac (Inguinal) | Hypogastric (Pubic) | Left Iliac (Inguinal) |

---

# 2. Layered Topography (Superficial to Deep)
If you "stab" the abdomen with a needle, these are the layers in order:

1. **Skin**
2. **Superficial Fascia:** Divided into two (below the umbilicus):
    - *Camper’s fascia:* Fatty outer layer.
    - *Scarpa’s fascia:* Membranous inner layer.
3. **External Oblique muscle**
4. **Internal Oblique muscle**
5. **Transversus Abdominis muscle**
6. **Transversalis Fascia** (Deep fascia)
7. **Extraperitoneal fat**
8. **Parietal Peritoneum** (The innermost lining)

---

# 3. Muscles of the Antero-lateral Wall
There are 3 flat muscles and 1 vertical muscle.

## A. Flat Muscles (Lateral)
1. **External Oblique:**
    - **Direction:** Fibers run "hands in pockets" (down and medially).
    - **Note:** Its lower border forms the **Inguinal Ligament**.
2. **Internal Oblique:**
    - **Direction:** Fibers run "up and medially" (perpendicular to external).
3. **Transversus Abdominis:**
    - **Direction:** Fibers run horizontally.
    - **Function:** Compresses abdominal contents.

## B. Vertical Muscle (Anterior)
1. **Rectus Abdominis:**
    - The "six-pack" muscle.
    - Runs vertically from the pubic symphysis to the xiphoid process/ribs.
    - Enclosed in the **Rectus Sheath** (formed by the aponeuroses of the 3 flat muscles).

---

# 4. Essential High-Yield Facts
- **Linea Alba:** The midline "white line" where the muscle aponeuroses meet (no major blood vessels here, good for surgery).
- **Innervation:** All muscles are supplied by the **lower 6 thoracic nerves (T7-T12)**.
- **Functions:** 
    1. Protect abdominal organs.
    2. Increase intra-abdominal pressure (coughing, vomiting, defecation).
    3. Move the trunk (flexion and rotation).

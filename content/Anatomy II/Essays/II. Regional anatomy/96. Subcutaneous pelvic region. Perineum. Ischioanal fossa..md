> Subcutaneous pelvic region. Perineum. Ischioanal fossa.

---

# 1. The Perineum (The Diamond)
- **Shape:** Diamond-shaped area between the thighs.
- **Boundaries:** 
    - **Anterior:** Pubic symphysis.
    - **Posterior:** Coccyx.
    - **Lateral:** Ischial tuberosities.
- **Division:** Divided by an imaginary line between the ischial tuberosities into:
    1. **Urogenital Triangle (Anterior):** Contains external genitalia and urethra.
    2. **Anal Triangle (Posterior):** Contains the anus and ischioanal fossae.

# 2. Subcutaneous Tissue (Fascia)
- **Superficial layer:** Fatty (Camper’s fascia).
- **Deep layer:** Membranous (**Colles’ fascia**). 
- **Clinical Pearl:** Colles’ fascia is continuous with **Scarpa’s fascia** of the abdomen. If the male urethra ruptures, urine can leak into the scrotum and up the abdominal wall, but it *cannot* go into the thighs (because the fascia attaches to the fascia lata of the leg).

# 3. Ischioanal Fossa (Ischiorectal Fossa)
This is the "fat-filled" wedge space on either side of the anal canal.

- **Function:** The fat allows the anal canal to expand during a bowel movement.
- **Boundaries:**
    - **Lateral wall:** Obturator internus muscle and the Ischial tuberosity.
    - **Medial wall:** Levator ani muscle and External anal sphincter.
- **Contents:**
    1. **Ischioanal fat pad** (large amount of fat).
    2. **Pudendal (Alcock’s) Canal:** A tunnel on the lateral wall.
    3. **Pudendal nerve** and **Internal pudendal vessels** (these provide the main nerve/blood supply to the perineum).

# Summary Checklist for "Points"
- Diamond shape? **Yes.**
- Ischioanal fossa contains fat? **Yes.**
- Pudendal nerve is the main nerve? **Yes.**
- Pudendal canal is on the lateral wall (Obturator internus)? **Yes.**
- Colles' fascia is continuous with abdominal fascia? **Yes.**

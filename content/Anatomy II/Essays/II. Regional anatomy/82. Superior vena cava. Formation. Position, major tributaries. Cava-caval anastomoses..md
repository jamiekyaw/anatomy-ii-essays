> Superior vena cava. Formation. Position, major tributaries. Cava-caval anastomoses.

---

# 1. Formation
- **Formed by:** Union of the **Right and Left Brachiocephalic veins**.
- **Location:** Behind the **1st right costal cartilage**.

![|400](https://teachmeanatomy.info/wp-content/uploads/Anatomical-Position-of-the-Superior-Vena-Cava-in-the-Thorax.png)

# 2. Position and Course
- **Length:** Approximately 7 cm long.
- **Course:** Descends vertically behind the right side of the sternum.
- **Termination:** Enters the **Right Atrium** of the heart at the level of the **3rd right costal cartilage**.
- **Valves:** It has **no valves** (blood flows via gravity).

![|400](https://teachmeanatomy.info/wp-content/uploads/Anatomical-Position-of-the-Superior-Vena-Cava-in-the-Thorax.png)
# 3. Major Tributaries
- **Azygos Vein:** The most important tributary. It arches over the root of the right lung to join the SVC just before it enters the heart.
- **Small vessels:** Pericardial veins and Mediastinal veins.

# 4. Cava-Caval Anastomoses
These are "backup routes" that connect the SVC system to the **Inferior Vena Cava (IVC)** system. If one is blocked, blood can still reach the heart.

- **Route 1: Surface (Anterolateral wall)**
    - **Thoracoepigastric vein:** Connects the Lateral Thoracic vein (SVC system) to the Superficial Epigastric vein (IVC system).
- **Route 2: Deep (Posterior wall)**
    - **Azygos/Hemiazygos system:** Connects the SVC to the Lumbar veins (which go to the IVC).
- **Route 3: Vertebral system**
    - **Vertebral Venous Plexuses:** A network of veins inside and outside the spinal column that connects the two systems.

# Summary Checklist for Exam:
1. **Formation:** Left + Right Brachiocephalic veins.
2. **Level:** Starts at 1st costal cartilage, ends at 3rd.
3. **Tributary:** Azygos vein.
4. **Clinical:** If SVC is blocked, Thoracoepigastric veins enlarge (visible on the skin).

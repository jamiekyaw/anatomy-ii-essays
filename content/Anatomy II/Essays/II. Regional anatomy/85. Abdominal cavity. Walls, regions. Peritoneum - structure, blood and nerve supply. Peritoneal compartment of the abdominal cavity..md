> Abdominal cavity. Walls, regions. Peritoneum - structure, blood and nerve supply. Peritoneal compartment of the abdominal cavity.

---

# 1. The Abdominal Cavity: Walls
The cavity is bounded by:
- **Superior (Top):** Diaphragm.
- **Inferior (Bottom):** Continuous with the pelvic cavity (at the pelvic inlet).
- **Posterior (Back):** Lumbar vertebrae and muscles (Psoas, Quadratus lumborum).
- **Anterolateral (Front/Sides):** Abdominal muscles (Rectus abdominis, External/Internal obliques, Transversus abdominis).

# 2. Regions (The 9-Region Grid)
Standard medical division using two vertical (midclavicular) and two horizontal lines:
1. **Right Hypochondriac** (Liver, Gallbladder)
2. **Epigastric** (Stomach, Pancreas)
3. **Left Hypochondriac** (Spleen)
4. **Right Lumbar/Flank** (Ascending colon)
5. **Umbilical** (Small intestine)
6. **Left Lumbar/Flank** (Descending colon)
7. **Right Iliac/Inguinal** (Appendix, Cecum)
8. **Hypogastric/Pubic** (Bladder, Uterus)
9. **Left Iliac/Inguinal** (Sigmoid colon)

# 3. Peritoneum: Structure
A serous membrane divided into two layers:
1. **Parietal Peritoneum:** Lines the internal surface of the abdominal wall.
2. **Visceral Peritoneum:** Covers the actual organs (viscera).
3. **Peritoneal Cavity:** The potential space between these layers containing a thin film of lubricating fluid.

![|400](https://teachmeanatomy.info/wp-content/uploads/Overview-of-the-Peritoneal-Abdominal-Cavity.png)

**Key Terms:**
- **Intraperitoneal:** Organs almost completely covered by peritoneum (e.g., Stomach, Spleen).
- **Retroperitoneal:** Organs behind the peritoneum, only covered on one side (e.g., Kidneys, Pancreas).
- **Mesentery:** Double layer of peritoneum that attaches organs to the posterior wall (carries blood vessels).
- **Omentum:** Folds of peritoneum. **Greater Omentum** (hangs from stomach like an apron); **Lesser Omentum** (connects stomach to liver).

# 4. Blood and Nerve Supply (High Yield)
This is the most common exam question:

- **Nerve Supply:**
    - **Parietal Peritoneum:** Supplied by **Somatic nerves** (Phrenic and Intercostal nerves). It is **highly sensitive** to pain, pressure, and temperature. Pain is well-localized.
    - **Visceral Peritoneum:** Supplied by **Autonomic nerves**. It is **insensitive** to touch/temp; it only responds to stretch or chemical irritation. Pain is poorly localized (referred pain).
- **Blood Supply:**
    - **Parietal:** Same as the abdominal wall (Intercostal/Epigastric arteries).
    - **Visceral:** Same as the organs it covers (Celiac, Superior/Inferior mesenteric arteries).

---

# 5. Peritoneal Compartments
The peritoneal cavity is divided into two main sacs:
1. **Greater Sac:** The main, larger part of the cavity.
2. **Lesser Sac (Omental Bursa):** A small space located **behind the stomach** and liver. It allows the stomach to move freely.

**Communication:**
- The Greater and Lesser sacs communicate via a single hole called the **Epiploic Foramen (Foramen of Winslow)**.

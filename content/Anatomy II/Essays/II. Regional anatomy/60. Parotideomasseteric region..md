> Parotideomasseteric region.

---
# 1. Boundaries (Where is it?)
- **Superior:** Zygomatic arch (cheekbone).
- **Inferior:** Lower border of the mandible (jawline).
- **Anterior:** Anterior border of the Masseter muscle.
- **Posterior:** External ear and Sternocleidomastoid muscle.

# 2. The Masseter Muscle
- **Action:** It is a muscle of mastication (chewing). It **elevates the mandible** (closes the mouth).
- **Nerve:** Mandibular nerve (CN V3).

# 3. The Parotid Gland (Key Structure)
- The largest salivary gland. 
- **Parotid Duct (Stensen’s Duct):** Runs across the masseter, pierces the buccinator muscle, and opens into the mouth opposite the **upper 2nd molar**.

# 4. Structures Passing THROUGH the Parotid Gland
*This is the most common exam question. Remember them from superficial to deep:*
1. **Facial Nerve (CN VII):** Divides into 5 branches inside the gland (Temporal, Zygomatic, Buccal, Marginal Mandibular, Cervical). *Note: It does NOT nerve-supply the gland, it just passes through.*
2. **Retromandibular Vein.**
3. **External Carotid Artery:** Divides into its terminal branches (Maxillary and Superficial Temporal arteries).

# 5. Nerve Supply to the Gland (Autonomics)
- **Sensory:** Auriculotemporal nerve.
- **Secretomotor (Parasympathetic):** Glossopharyngeal nerve (**CN IX**) $\rightarrow$ Otic ganglion $\rightarrow$ Auriculotemporal nerve. (This makes the saliva flow).

---

# Summary Checklist for the Exam:
- **Muscle:** Masseter (Closes jaw).
- **Gland:** Parotid.
- **Duct:** Opens at upper 2nd molar.
- **Nerve inside:** Facial Nerve (CN VII) – *Damage here causes Bell's Palsy/facial drooping.*
- **Artery inside:** External Carotid.

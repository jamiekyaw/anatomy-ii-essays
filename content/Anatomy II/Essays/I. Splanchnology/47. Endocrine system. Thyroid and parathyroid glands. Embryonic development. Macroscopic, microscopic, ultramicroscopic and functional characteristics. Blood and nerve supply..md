> Endocrine system. Thyroid and parathyroid glands. Embryonic development. Macroscopic, microscopic, ultramicroscopic and functional characteristics. Blood and nerve supply.

---

# 1. Embryonic Development
- **Thyroid:**
    - Originates from **endoderm** at the floor of the pharynx (**Foramen Cecum**).
    - Descends via the **Thyroglossal Duct** to the neck (C5-T1).
    - *Clinical:* Remnants can form Thyroglossal cysts.
- **Parathyroid:**
    - **Superior Glands:** From **4th** pharyngeal pouch.
    - **Inferior Glands:** From **3rd** pharyngeal pouch (descend with the Thymus; hence they end up lower).

---

# 2. Macroscopic Anatomy
- **Thyroid:**
    - **Shape:**
	    - Butterfly/H-shape.
	    - Two lateral lobes connected by an **isthmus** (over 2nd–4th tracheal rings).
    - **Pyramidal Lobe:** A common anatomical variant (remnant of thyroglossal duct) extending upward from the isthmus.
    - **Capsule:** Inner true capsule and outer false capsule (pretracheal fascia).
- **Parathyroid:**
    - 4 small, oval, yellowish-brown glands located on the **posterior** surface of the thyroid lobes.

---

# 3. Microscopic Anatomy (Histology)
- **Thyroid:**
    - **Follicles:**
	    - The structural unit.
	    - Spheres filled with **Colloid** (stored thyroglobulin).
    - **Follicular Cells:**
	    - Simple cuboidal (inactive) to columnar (active).
	    - Produce T3/T4.
    - **Parafollicular (C-cells):**
	    - Large, pale cells between follicles.
	    - Produce **Calcitonin**.
- **Parathyroid:**
    - **Chief Cells:**
	    - Small, dark-staining, most numerous.
	    - Produce **PTH** (Parathyroid Hormone).
    - **Oxyphil Cells:**
	    - Larger, acidophilic (pink), appear after puberty.
	    - Function unknown.

---

# 4. Ultramicroscopic (EM) Characteristics
- **Follicular Cells:**
    - Abundant **Rough ER** and **Golgi** (for protein synthesis).
    - **Apical Microvilli** (to increase surface area for iodine uptake/colloid resorption).
    - **Lysosomes** (to break down thyroglobulin into T3/T4).
- **Chief Cells:**
    - Dense secretory granules containing PTH.

---

# 5. Functional Characteristics
- **T3 (Triiodothyronine) & T4 (Thyroxine):**
    - Regulate Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR), heat production, and growth/development.
    - Stimulated by **TSH** from the pituitary.
- **Calcitonin:**
    - **Lowers** blood Calcium (inhibits osteoclasts). "Calcitonin *tones* down the calcium."
- **PTH (Parathyroid Hormone):**
    - **Increases** blood Calcium.
    - Actions: Bone (resorption), Kidney (reabsorb Ca2+), Gut (indirectly via Vitamin D).

---

# 6. Blood Supply and Nerve Supply
- **Arteries:**
    - **Superior Thyroid Artery:** 
	    - Branch of **External Carotid**.
    - **Inferior Thyroid Artery:**
	    - Branch of **Thyrocervical Trunk** (Subclavian).
    - *Note:* Parathyroids are supplied mainly by the **Inferior Thyroid Artery**.
- **Veins:**
    - Superior and Middle Thyroid Veins $\rightarrow$ **Internal Jugular Vein**.
    - Inferior Thyroid Vein $\rightarrow$ **Brachiocephalic Vein**.
- **Nerves:**
    - **Recurrent Laryngeal Nerve (RLN):**
	    - Runs behind the thyroid. *Critical:* Damage causes hoarseness or airway loss.
    - **External Laryngeal Nerve:**
	    - Supplies cricothyroid muscle.
    - Autonomics: Sympathetic (Vasomotor) and Parasympathetic (Vagus).

---

# "Must-Know" for Exams:
1. **Inverse Calcium Control:** PTH and Calcitonin are antagonists.
2. **Surgical Danger:** During thyroidectomy, the **Recurrent Laryngeal Nerve** and **Parathyroid glands** must be preserved to avoid voice loss and hypocalcemic tetany.
3. **Third Pouch Paradox:** The Inferior parathyroids come from the 3rd pouch (higher up initially) but travel further down than the Superior glands (4th pouch).

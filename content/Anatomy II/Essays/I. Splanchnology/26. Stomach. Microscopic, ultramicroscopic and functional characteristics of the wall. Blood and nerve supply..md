> Stomach. Microscopic, ultramicroscopic and functional characteristics of the wall. Blood and nerve supply.

---

# 1. Microscopic Characteristics (The 4 Layers)
The stomach follows the general GI plan but with specific modifications:

- **Mucosa:**
    - **Epithelium:**
	    - Simple columnar.
	    - Forms **gastric pits**.
    - **Lamina Propria:**
	    - Packed with gastric glands.
    - **Muscularis Mucosae:**
	    - Thin layer of smooth muscle to help gland emptying.
- **Submucosa:**
	- Dense irregular connective tissue.
	- Contains the **Meissner’s Plexus** (controls secretions).
- **Muscularis Externa:**
    1. Inner Oblique (unique to stomach - for churning).
    2. Middle Circular.
    3. Outer Longitudinal.
    - Contains **Auerbach’s (Myenteric) Plexus** between layers (controls motility).
- **Serosa:** Outer layer of visceral peritoneum.

# 2. Ultramicroscopic (Cell Types)

#### 1. Parietal Cells (Acid Producers)
- **Intracellular Canaliculi:**
	- Deep, branching channels that maximize surface area for acid release.
- **Tubulovesicular System:**
	- Cytoplasmic vesicles
	- Fuse with canaliculi when active to insert proton pumps.
- **Abundant Mitochondria:**
	- Occupy ~40% of cell volume to supply $\text{ATP}$ for pumping hydrogen ions.
- **Apical Microvilli:**
	- Project into canaliculi to expand secretory surface area.

![](https://ars.els-cdn.com/content/image/3-s2.0-B9780128158449000063-f06-15-9780128158449.jpg)

#### 2. Chief Cells (Enzyme Producers)
- **Extensive Rough Endoplasmic Reticulum:**
	- Heavily packed at the cell base for high-volume protein (pepsinogen) synthesis.
- **Zymogen Granules:**
	- Large storage vesicles crowded at the top (apex), holding inactive pepsinogen.
- **Prominent Golgi:**
	- Well-developed complex for packaging enzymes into granules.
    
![|300](https://www.shutterstock.com/image-vector/chief-cell-stomach-wall-vector-600nw-2481813463.jpg)
#### 3. Mucous Cells (Protective Shield)
- **Short Microvilli & Glycocalyx:**
	- Help anchor the protective mucus layer to the cell surface.
- **Strict Tight Junctions:**
	- Seals between cells that prevent acid from leaking into deeper tissues.

# 3. Functional
Located within the gastric glands:

- **Mucous Neck Cells:**
	- Secrete acidic mucus (different from surface alkaline mucus).
- **Parietal Cells:**
    - *Function:* Secrete **HCl** acid and **Intrinsic Factor** (Vitamin B12 absorption).
- **Chief Cells:**
    - *Function:* Secrete **Pepsinogen** (inactive) and Gastric Lipase.
- **Enteroendocrine Cells (G-Cells):**
    - *Function:* Secrete hormone **Gastrin** into the blood to stimulate acid production.
- **Stem Cells:**
	- Located in the "isthmus/neck" region.
	- Replace the lining every 3-5 days

---

# 4. Blood Supply
All arterial supply comes directly or indirectly from the **Celiac Trunk**.

- **Lesser Curvature:**
    - Left Gastric Artery.
    - Right Gastric Artery.
- **Greater Curvature:**
    - Left Gastro-omental.
    - Right Gastro-omental.
- **Fundus:**
    - Short Gastric Arteries.

**Venous Drainage:** All drain eventually into the **Portal Vein**.

---

# 5. Nerve Supply
- **Parasympathetic (Rest & Digest):**
    - **Vagus Nerve (CN X).**
    - *Function:* Increases peristalsis, relaxes sphincters, and stimulates acid secretion.
- **Sympathetic (Fight or Flight):**
    - **Celiac Plexus (T5–T9).**
    - *Function:* Inhibits peristalsis, constricts blood vessels to the stomach.
- **Enteric Nervous System (Intrinsic):**
    - **Meissner’s Plexus:** Controls secretions and blood flow.
    - **Auerbach’s Plexus:** Controls muscle contractions (motility).

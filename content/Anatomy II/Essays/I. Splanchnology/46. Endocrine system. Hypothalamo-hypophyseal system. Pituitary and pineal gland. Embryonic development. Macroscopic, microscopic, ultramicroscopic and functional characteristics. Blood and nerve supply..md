> Endocrine system. Hypothalamo-hypophyseal system. Pituitary and pineal gland. Embryonic development. Macroscopic, microscopic, ultramicroscopic and functional characteristics. Blood and nerve supply.

---

# 1. Hypothalamo-Hypophyseal System
The functional link between the brain and the endocrine system.

- **Components:** Hypothalamus and Pituitary gland.
- **Neural Connection:** **Hypothalamo-hypophyseal tract** (Axons from Supraoptic/Paraventricular nuclei to Posterior Pituitary).
- **Vascular Connection:** **Hypophyseal portal system** (Blood vessels carrying "releasing hormones" from hypothalamus to Anterior Pituitary).

# 2. Pituitary Gland (Hypophysis)

## A. Embryonic Development
- **Adenohypophysis (Anterior):** Derived from **Rathke’s Pouch** (Ectoderm of the primitive oral cavity).
- **Neurohypophysis (Posterior):** Derived from the **Infundibulum** (Neuroectoderm of the floor of the Diencephalon).

## B. Macroscopic Anatomy
- **Location:** Sits in the **Sella turcica** of the sphenoid bone.
- **Parts:** 
    - Anterior Lobe: Pars distalis, Pars tuberalis, Pars intermedia.
    - Posterior Lobe: Pars nervosa, Infundibular stalk.

## C. Microscopic & Ultramicroscopic
- **Anterior Lobe (Chromophils):**
    1. **Acidophils (GPA):** **G**rowth Hormone (Somatotropes), **P**rolactin (Lactotropes).
    2. **Basophils (B-FLAT):** **F**SH, **L**H (Gonadotropes), **A**CTH (Corticotropes), **T**SH (Thyrotropes).
- **Posterior Lobe:**
    - Not a gland (a storage site).
    - **Pituicytes:** Supporting glial cells.
    - **Herring Bodies:** Dilated axon terminals containing hormones.
- **Ultramicroscopic:** Abundant RER, Golgi apparatus, and **membrane-bound secretory granules**.

## D. Functional Characteristics
- **Anterior:** Produces hormones that regulate growth, metabolism, and reproduction.
- **Posterior:** Releases **ADH** (water retention) and **Oxytocin** (milk let-down/uterine contraction). Produced in the hypothalamus.

## E. Blood & Nerve Supply
- **Blood:** **Superior hypophyseal artery** (forms portal system for Anterior); **Inferior hypophyseal artery** (supplies Posterior).
- **Nerve:** Hypothalamo-hypophyseal tract (Neural); Vasomotor sympathetic fibers for blood vessels.

# 3. Pineal Gland (Epiphysis Cerebri)

## A. Embryonic Development
- Derived from **Neuroectoderm** of the roof of the diencephalon (back of the 3rd ventricle).

## B. Macroscopic Anatomy
- Small, pine-cone shaped structure.
- Located in the midline, attached to the posterior wall of the third ventricle.

## C. Microscopic & Ultramicroscopic
- **Pinealocytes:** Primary cells, arranged in cords. Produce Melatonin.
- **Interstitial Cells:** Glial-like support cells.
- **Corpora Arenacea (Brain Sand):** Calcified concretions (increase with age; useful as midline markers on X-rays).
- **Ultramicroscopic:** Feature **"Synaptic Ribbons"** and dense-core vesicles for melatonin secretion.

## D. Functional Characteristics
- **Melatonin:** Regulates **Circadian Rhythm** (sleep-wake cycle).
- Inhibits reproductive function in some species (antigonadotropic).
- Responsive to light/dark cycles via the retina.

## E. Blood & Nerve Supply
- **Blood:** Branches of the **Posterior Cerebral Artery**.
- **Nerve:** **Sympathetic fibers** originating from the **Superior Cervical Ganglion**. This is the "pathway of light": Retina $\rightarrow$ Hypothalamus $\rightarrow$ Spinal Cord $\rightarrow$ Superior Cervical Ganglion $\rightarrow$ Pineal Gland.

> Digestive system- the major organs of the digestive system. Principal structure of tubular and parenchymatous organs

---

# 1. Classification of Organs
In the digestive system, organs are classified into two structural types:
1. **Tubular Organs:**
	- These form the **Alimentary Canal**
	- It is a continuous tube from mouth to anus.
2. **Parenchymatous Organs:**
	- These are the **Accessory Glands**
	- They secrete enzymes and fluids into the tube.

---

# 2. Major Organs: The Alimentary Canal (Tubular)

## A. Esophagus
- **Structure:** Straight muscular tube.
- **Function:** Transport of food via peristalsis.
- **Key Note:** The only part of the digestive tube with skeletal muscle (top 1/3) transitioning to smooth muscle.
## B. Stomach
- **Structure:** J-shaped sac; has an extra muscle layer for churning.
- **Mucosa:** Folded into **Rugae** (allow expansion).
- **Function:** Chemical breakdown (HCl and Pepsin) and mechanical "chyme" production.
## C. Small Intestine (Duodenum, Jejunum, Ileum)
- **Structure:** Designed for massive surface area.
- **Features:** **Villi** (finger-like projections) and **Microvilli** (brush border).
- **Function:** 90% of all nutrient absorption occurs here.
## D. Large Intestine (Colon)
- **Structure:** Wider diameter; lacks villi. 
- **Features:** Contains **Tenis coli** (muscle bands) and **Haustra** (pouches).
- **Function:** Water reabsorption and feces formation.

---

# 3. Major Organs: Accessory Glands (Parenchymatous)

## A. Salivary Glands (Parotid, Submandibular, Sublingual)
- **Function:** Produce saliva containing **Amylase** (breaks down starch).

## B. The Liver
- **Structure:** Largest gland; organized into **Hexagonal Lobules**.
- **Functional unit:** The Hepatocyte.
- **Blood supply:** Unique "Portal System" (receives blood from the gut to filter it).
- **Function:** Produces **Bile** (emulsifies fats) and detoxifies blood.

## C. The Pancreas
- **Type:** A "Mixed" gland (Exocrine and Endocrine).
- **Exocrine part (Acinar cells):** Secretes digestive enzymes (trypsin, lipase) into the duodenum.
- **Endocrine part (Islets of Langerhans):** Secretes Insulin and Glucagon into the blood.

## D. Gallbladder
- **Structure:** Small green sac under the liver.
- **Function:** Stores and concentrates bile. (It does *not* produce bile).

# 4. General Structure of Tubular Organs
Most tubular organs (esophagus, stomach, intestines) share a consistent **four-layered wall structure**. From the inside (lumen) out, they are:

1. **Mucosa (Inmost layer):**
    - Epithelium: Varies by location (e.g., protective in the esophagus, absorptive in the intestines).
    - Lamina Propria: Connective tissue with blood vessels and lymph nodes.
    - Muscularis Mucosae: A thin layer of smooth muscle that creates folds.
2. **Submucosa:** 
    - Dense irregular connective tissue containing larger blood vessels, lymphatics, and the **Meissner’s Plexus** (controls secretions).
3. **Muscularis Externa:**
    - Usually two layers of smooth muscle: **Inner circular** and **Outer longitudinal**.
    - Contains the **Auerbach’s (Myenteric) Plexus** (controls peristalsis/movement).
4. **Serosa or Adventitia (Outmost layer):**
    - Serosa: If the organ is within the peritoneal cavity (slippery membrane).
    - Adventitia: If the organ is outside the cavity (fibrous connective tissue, e.g., esophagus).

---

# 5. General Structure of Parenchymatous Organs
Solid organs (Liver, Pancreas, Salivary Glands) do not have a lumen. Instead, they consist of:

1. **Stroma (The Framework):**
    - **Capsule:** The outer connective tissue covering.
    - **Septa/Trabeculae:** Connective tissue walls that dive into the organ to divide it into **Lobes** and **Lobules**.
    - **Ret fibers:** A fine mesh supporting individual cells.
2. **Parenchyma (The Functional Part):**
    - The actual cells that perform the organ's work (e.g., Hepatocytes in the liver, Acinar cells in the pancreas).

## Exam Tip: 
If asked about the **"Tubular structure,"** always list the **4 layers**. If asked about **"Parenchymatous structure,"** always mention the **Capsule, Septa, and Lobules.**

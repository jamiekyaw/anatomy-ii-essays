> Trachea and main (principal) bronchi. Macroscopic, microscopic, ultramicroscopic and functional characteristics. Blood and nerve supply.

---

# 1. Macroscopic Characteristics
## The Trachea (The Windpipe):
- **Location:**
	- Starts at **C6** (lower border of cricoid cartilage), ends at **T4/T5** (Sternal angle).
- **Length:**
	- ~10–12 cm.
- **Structure:**
	- 16–20 **C-shaped hyaline cartilage rings**. 
- **Trachealis Muscle:**
	- Smooth muscle closing the "C" posteriorly (allows esophagus to expand during swallowing).
- **Carina:**
	- Internal ridge at the bifurcation (most sensitive area for cough reflex).

## The Main Bronchi (Right vs. Left):
- **Right Main Bronchus:**
	- Wider, shorter (2.5 cm), and more **vertical**.
	- *Clinical: Foreign bodies usually lodge here.*
- **Left Main Bronchus:**
	- Narrower, longer (5 cm), and more horizontal.

---

# 2. Microscopic Characteristics
The wall consists of four distinct layers:
1.  **Mucosa:** 
    - **Epithelium:** Respiratory epithelium (**Pseudostratified ciliated columnar**).
    - **Lamina Propria:** Elastic fiber-rich connective tissue.
2.  **Submucosa:** Contains **seromucous glands** (produce fluid/mucus).
3.  **Musculo-cartilaginous layer:** Hyaline cartilage rings and trachealis muscle.
4.  **Adventitia:** Outermost connective tissue binding it to the neck/mediastinum.

# 3. Ultramicroscopic Characteristics
- **Ciliated Cells:** Move mucus toward the pharynx ("Mucociliary escalator").
- **Goblet Cells:** Secrete mucus to trap dust/bacteria.
- **Basal Cells:** Stem cells for epithelium replacement.
- **Brush Cells:** Sensory cells (chemoreceptors).
- **K-cells (Kulchitsky):** Part of the diffuse neuroendocrine system (hormone secretion).

---

# 4. Functional Characteristics
- **Air Conduction:** Low-resistance pathway for air.
- **Protection:** Mucociliary escalator and cough reflex remove debris.
- **Conditioning:** Warms and humidifies inspired air.
- **Patency:** Cartilage prevents collapse under negative pressure during inhalation.

---

# 5. Blood and Nerve Supply
**Arterial Supply:**
- **Upper part (Trachea):** Inferior thyroid arteries.
- **Lower part (Bronchi):** Bronchial arteries (branches of the thoracic aorta).

**Venous Drainage:**
- Drains into the
	- Inferior thyroid venous plexus 
	- Azygos/Hemiazygos veins.

**Nerve Supply:**
- **Parasympathetic:**
	- Vagus Nerve (CN X) via Recurrent Laryngeal Nerve.
	- *Function: Bronchoconstriction and secretion.*
- **Sympathetic:**
	- Sympathetic Trunk (T1–T4).
	- *Function: Bronchodilation.*

> Pancreas. Embryonic development. Macroscopic, microscopic, ultramicroscopic and functional characteristics of exocrine and endocrine part. Blood and nerve supply.

---

# 1. General Overview
- **Type:** A "heterocrine" gland (both exocrine and endocrine).
- **Location:**
	- Retroperitoneal (except the tail),
	- Situated transversely across the posterior abdominal wall at the level of L1-L2 vertebrae.

---

# 2. Embryonic Development
- **Origin:** Derived from the **endodermal lining** of the foregut.
- **Developmental Buds:**
    - **Ventral Pancreatic Bud:**
	    - Develops near the entry of the bile duct.
	    - Forms the **uncinate process** and the **lower part of the head**.
    - **Dorsal Pancreatic Bud:**
	    - Larger, develops from the dorsal mesentery.
	    - Forms the **upper head, neck, body, and tail**.
- **Rotation:**
	- During the rotation of the duodenum, the ventral bud moves posteriorly to fuse with the dorsal bud.
- **Duct Development:** 
    - The **Main Pancreatic Duct (Wirsung)**:
	    - Formed by the union of the distal part of the dorsal duct and the entire ventral duct.
    - The proximal part of the dorsal duct:
	    - Becomes the **Accessory Pancreatic Duct (Santorini)**.

## 3. Macroscopic Characteristics (Gross Anatomy)
The pancreas is divided into five parts:
-  **Head**
-  **Uncinate Process**
-  **Neck**
-  **Body**
-  **Tail**

**The Duct System:**
- **Main Duct:**
	- Joins the Common Bile Duct to form the **Ampulla of Vater**
	- Opens at the Major Duodenal Papilla.
- **Accessory Duct:**
	- Opens into the Minor Duodenal Papilla (2cm above the major).

# 4. Microscopic Characteristics

## A. Exocrine Part (98% of the gland)
Composed of *serous acini.*
- **Acini:**
	- Grape-like clusters of pyramidal epithelial cells. 
- **Staining:**
	- Highly basophilic base (due to RER) and acidophilic apex (due to zymogen granules).
- **Centroacinar cells:**
	- Unique to the pancreas
	- These are the start of the intercalated ducts
	- Found in the center of the acinus.

## B. Endocrine Part (1-2% of the gland)
**Islets of Langerhans:**
- Pale-staining spherical clusters of cells scattered among the dark acini
- Most numerous in the tail
- Supported by a fine network of reticular fibers and fenestrated capillaries.

# 5. Ultramicroscopic Characteristics

## A. Exocrine Part (98% of the gland)
* **Rough Endoplasmic Reticulum (RER):**
	- Extremely well-developed in the basal region for protein synthesis.
    * **Golgi Complex:**
	    * Large, for packaging enzymes.
    * **Zymogen Granules:**
	    * Dense, membrane-bound vesicles in the apical region containing inactive digestive enzymes.

## Endocrine Part (1-2% of the gland)
- **Alpha cells:**
	- Located peripherally. Granules have a dense core and narrow halo.
-  **Beta cells:**
	- Located centrally. Granules have an **irregular/crystalline core** and a wide halo.
-  **Delta cells:**
	- Large, pale granules.
-  **PP cells:**
	- Rare; contain small, dark granules.

# 6. Functional Characteristics

## A. Exocrine Function
- Secretes **Pancreatic Juice** (1.5L/day),
- Regulated by the hormones Secretin and Cholecystokinin.
- **Bicarbonate:** Secreted by duct cells to neutralize stomach acid.
- **Enzymes:** 
    - *Proteases (break down proteins):*
	    - Trypsinogen,
	    - Chymotrypsinogen.
    - *Amylase:* Digests carbohydrates.
    - *Lipase:* Digests fats.

## B. Endocrine Function
Hormones are secreted directly into the bloodstream:
- **Insulin (Beta cells):**
	- Lowers blood glucose (promotes glucose uptake by cells).
- **Glucagon (Alpha cells):**
	- Raises blood glucose (promotes glycogenolysis in the liver).
- **Somatostatin (Delta cells):**
	- Inhibits the release of both insulin and glucagon;
	- Slows GI motility.
- **Pancreatic Polypeptide:** Inhibits exocrine pancreatic secretion.

---

# 6. Blood and Nerve Supply

## Arterial Supply
The pancreas has a rich, dual blood supply from the:
- Celiac Trunk
- Superior Mesenteric Artery:
1. **Head and Neck:**
	- Superior and Inferior Pancreaticoduodenal arteries
2. **Body and Tail:**
	- Splenic artery
## Venous Drainage
-  Veins follow the arteries.
- Ultimately drain into the **Portal Vein**
	- via the Splenic and Superior Mesenteric veins.
	- This ensures hormones like insulin go directly to the liver first.
## Nerve Supply
- **Parasympathetic:**
	- **Vagus Nerve (CN X)**.
	- Stimulates both exocrine secretion and insulin release.
- **Sympathetic:**
	- **Celiac and Superior Mesenteric Plexuses** (T6-T10).
	- Primarily vasomotor (constricts blood vessels) and carries pain fibers.

> Oral cavity. Proper oral cavity. Hard palate, soft palate, oral diaphragm, oropharyngeal isthmus. Macroscopic and microscopic characteristics. Blood and nerve supply.

---

# 1. The Oral Cavity Proper
The space internal to the dental arches.
- **Boundaries:**
    - **Anterolateral:** Teeth and gingiva (gums).
    - **Superior (Roof):** Hard and soft palates.
    - **Inferior (Floor):** Tongue and the oral diaphragm.
    - **Posterior:** Oropharyngeal isthmus (communicates with the oropharynx).

# 2. The Roof: Hard and Soft Palates

## A. Hard Palate (Anterior 2/3)
- **Macroscopic:** Formed by
	- Palatine processes of the Maxilla
	- Horizontal plates of the Palatine bones
	- Features include the incisive papilla and transverse palatine rugae (ridges).
- **Microscopic:** **Masticatory mucosa**
	- Keratinized stratified squamous epithelium.
	- Firmly bound to periosteum.
	- Contains minor salivary glands in the posterior part.
- **Blood Supply:** Greater palatine artery (branch of Maxillary artery).
- **Nerve Supply:** Greater palatine nerve and Nasopalatine nerve (both from CN V2).

![](https://nci-media.cancer.gov/pdq/media/images/716338-571.jpg)

## B. Soft Palate (Posterior 1/3)
- **Macroscopic:**
	- A mobile muscular fold. Ends in the **uvula**.
- **Microscopic:** **Lining mucosa**
	- Non-keratinized stratified squamous epithelium.
	- Contains a core of skeletal muscle, palatine aponeurosis, and mucous glands.
- **Muscles:**
    1. *Levator veli palatini*
    2. *Tensor veli palatini*
    3. *Palatoglossus*
    4. *Palatopharyngeus*
    5. *Musculus uvulae*
- **Blood Supply:** Lesser palatine artery, Ascending palatine (from Facial artery).
- **Nerve Supply:**
    - **Motor:** Pharyngeal plexus (CN X) — **Note:** Tensor veli palatini is the exception (CN V3).
    - **Sensory:** Lesser palatine nerves (CN V2).

# 3. The Floor: Oral Diaphragm
- **Macroscopic:** The soft tissue area beneath the tongue.
- **Key Muscle:** **Mylohyoid muscle**. It forms the anatomical "diaphragm" or floor of the mouth.
- **Supporting Muscles:** Geniohyoid (above mylohyoid) and Digastric (below mylohyoid).
- **Structures found here:** Sublingual glands, submandibular ducts, lingual nerve, and lingual artery.
- **Nerve Supply (Mylohyoid):** Nerve to mylohyoid (branch of CN V3).

---

# 4. Oropharyngeal Isthmus (Fauces)
The "doorway" between the mouth and the throat.
- **Boundaries:**
    - **Superior:** Soft palate.
    - **Inferior:** Root of the tongue.
    - **Lateral:** **Palatoglossal arch** (Anterior pillar) and **Palatopharyngeal arch** (Posterior pillar).
- **Clinical Note:** The palatine tonsils sit in the "tonsillar sinus" between these two arches.

---

# 5. Summary of Histology (General)
- **Type of Epithelium:** Mostly **Non-keratinized stratified squamous epithelium** (lining mucosa).
- **Exceptions:** Hard palate and Gingiva are **Keratinized** (masticatory mucosa) to withstand the friction of chewing.
- **Lamina Propria:** Connective tissue layer beneath the epithelium containing blood vessels and nerves.

# Summary for Quick Revision

| Structure | Main Artery | Main Sensory Nerve |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Upper Oral Cavity** | Greater Palatine (Maxillary) | CN V2 (Maxillary Div.) |
| **Lower Oral Cavity** | Lingual & Inferior Alveolar | CN V3 (Mandibular Div.) |
| **Cheeks** | Buccal Artery | Buccal Nerve (CN V3) |
| **Soft Palate** | Lesser Palatine | Lesser Palatine (CN V2) |

**Important Rule for Motor Supply:**
- **All muscles of the Palate** are supplied by the **Vagus Nerve (CN X)** via the pharyngeal plexus, EXCEPT the **Tensor Veli Palatini (CN V3)**.
- **All muscles of the Tongue** are supplied by the **Hypoglossal Nerve (CN XII)**, EXCEPT the **Palatoglossus (CN X)**.

# 1. WALL STRUCTURE

The uterine wall has three layers:

## A. Perimetrium (Outer)
- **Macroscopic:** Serosa (peritoneum) or adventitia.
- **Microscopic:** Simple squamous epithelium (mesothelium) and thin connective tissue.

## B. Myometrium (Middle)
- **Macroscopic:** Thickest layer; smooth muscle.
- **Microscopic:** Three poorly defined layers of smooth muscle (inner longitudinal, middle circular/vasculosa, outer longitudinal).
- **Ultramicroscopic:** Cells are joined by **gap junctions** (increase during pregnancy for coordinated labor).
- **Function:** Massive contraction during labor (induced by **Oxytocin**).

## C. Endometrium (Inner/Mucosa)
- **Microscopic:** Simple columnar epithelium (ciliated + secretory cells).
- **Two Layers:**
    1. **Stratum Functionale:** Thick, superficial; **sloughs off** during menstruation.
    2. **Stratum Basale:** Deep, permanent; **regenerates** the functional layer.
- **Function:** Site of embryo implantation.

# 2. OVARIO-MENSTRUAL CYCLE (Endometrial Phases)

The cycle is usually 28 days.

## Phase 1: Menstrual Phase (Days 1–4)
- **Trigger:** Drop in **Progesterone** and Estrogen.
- **Microscopic:** The **Stratum Functionale** sheds. Blood, tissue fluid, and epithelial cells are discharged.
- **Functional:** Clearing the "old" lining.

## Phase 2: Follicular Phase (Days 5–14)
- **Trigger:** **Estrogen** (from developing ovarian follicles).
- **Microscopic:** 
    - Glands are **straight and narrow**.
    - Stromal cells multiply; Stratum Functionale thickens.
- **Functional:** Rebuilding the lining after menstruation.

## Phase 3: Luteal Phase (Days 15–27)
- **Trigger:** **Progesterone** (from the Corpus Luteum).
- **Microscopic:** 
    - Glands become **coiled/saw-toothed** (tortuous).
    - Glands fill with **glycogen** (secretion).
    - Spiral arteries become fully developed and coiled.
- **Functional:** Preparing for embryo implantation ("uterine milk").

## Phase 4: Pre-menstrual Phase (Day 28)
- **Trigger:** Withdrawal of hormones.
- **Microscopic:** 
    - **Spiral arteries constrict** (spasms), cutting off blood flow.
    - Tissue begins to die (necrosis) before shedding.
- **Functional:** Preparation for the next menstrual flow.

# 3. BLOOD AND NERVE SUPPLY

## Blood Supply:
The hierarchy are as follows:
1. **Uterine Artery:** Branch of internal iliac artery.
2. **Arcuate Arteries:** Circle the myometrium.
3. **Radial Arteries:** Enter the endometrium.
4. **Straight Arteries:** Supply the Stratum Basale; do **not** shed.
5. **Spiral Arteries:** Supply the Stratum Functional; **shed** during menses.

## Nerve Supply:
- **Autonomic Nervous System:** Derived from the **Inferior Hypogastric Plexus**.
- **Sympathetic:** Vasoconstriction and muscular contraction.
- **Parasympathetic:** Vasodilation and muscular inhibition.
- **Sensory:** Pain fibers from the body of the uterus travel with sympathetic nerves (T10-L1).

> Lymphatic (immune) system. Lymph organs. Classification. General microscopic, ultramicroscopic and functional characteristics of the organs.

---

# 1. Classification

**Classification by Function:**
1. **Primary (Central) Lymphoid Organs:**
	- Where lymphocytes are **produced and matured**.
    - Bone Marrow: B-cell maturation.
    - Thymus: T-cell maturation.
2. **Secondary (Peripheral) Lymphoid Organs:**
	- Where lymphocytes **mount an immune response** (activation).
    - Lymph Nodes, Spleen, Tonsils, MALT (Mucosa-Associated Lymphoid Tissue), and Peyer’s patches.

# 2. General Microscopic Structure
Most lymphoid organs share a common structural plan:
- **Stroma (The Framework):**
	- Usually **Reticular Connective Tissue** (Type III collagen) produced by reticular cells. 
    - **Exception:** The **Thymus** uses an Epithelioreticular framework (no reticular fibers).
- **Parenchyma (The Functional Cells):**
	- Primarily Lymphocytes (T-cells, B-cells), Macrophages, and Dendritic cells.

---

# 3. The Primary Organs

### A. Thymus
- **Location:** Superior mediastinum.
- **Histology:** Divided into lobules by connective tissue septa.
    - **Cortex (Dark):** Densely packed with immature T-cells (thymocytes). 
    - **Medulla (Pale):** Fewer lymphocytes. Contains **Hassall’s Corpuscles** (concentric rings of epithelial cells)—*this is the "pass" keyword for identifying the thymus.*
- **Blood-Thymus Barrier:** Found only in the **cortex**. Prevents developing T-cells from being exposed to blood-borne antigens prematurely.
- **Function:** T-cell maturation and selection (eliminates self-reactive cells). Involution (shrinks and turns to fat) occurs after puberty.

![|500](https://embryology.med.unsw.edu.au/embryology/images/thumb/f/ff/Thymus_histology_06.jpg/600px-Thymus_histology_06.jpg)

### B. Bone Marrow
- **Function:** Site of hematopoiesis and B-lymphocyte maturation.

# 4. The Secondary Organs

### A. Lymph Nodes
- **Microscopic Structure:**
    1. **Cortex:** Contains Lymphatic Follicles (Nodules). Primary (inactive) vs. Secondary (active, with a pale **Germinal Center** where B-cells proliferate).
    2. **Paracortex:** The "T-cell zone." Contains **High Endothelial Venules** where lymphocytes exit the blood to enter the node.
    3. **Medulla:** Contains Medullary Cords (lymphocytes/plasma cells) and Medullary Sinuses.
- **Function:** Filter lymph fluid. Only organ with **Afferent** (incoming) and **Efferent** (outgoing) vessels.

![|500](https://medcell.org/systems_cell_biology/lymphatics_lab/images/lymph_node_labels.jpg)

### B. Spleen
- **Microscopic Structure:** No cortex/medulla. Divided into:
    1. **White Pulp:** Immune function. Contains the **PALS** (Periarteriolar Lymphoid Sheath) rich in T-cells, and Splenic Nodules (B-cells).
    2. **Red Pulp:** Blood filtration. Contains **Splenic Sinusoids** and **Splenic Cords (Cords of Billroth)**.
- **Function:** Filters blood (removes old RBCs) and mounts immune responses to blood-borne antigens.

![](https://www.anatomyatlases.org/MicroscopicAnatomy/Images/Plate172.jpg)

### C. MALT / Tonsils
- **MALT:** Lymphoid tissue in the gut (Peyer's patches), respiratory, and urogenital tracts.
- **Tonsils:** Incomplete capsules. Look for **crypts** (invaginations of epithelium) and lymphoid follicles.

---

# 5. Ultramicroscopic & Cell Characteristics

- **Lymphocytes:**
	- Small cells with a large, dark, heterochromatic nucleus and very little cytoplasm.
- **Plasma Cells (Active B-cells):** 
    - *Ultramicroscopic:* Abundant **Rough Endoplasmic Reticulum (RER)** for antibody synthesis. 
    - *Microscopic:* "Clock-face" or "Cartwheel" nucleus.
- **Macrophages:**
	- Contain many **lysosomes** and phagosomes for digesting debris.
- **Dendritic Cells:**
	- Large branched processes to "present" antigens to T-cells.

# Exam "Cheat Sheet" Tips:
1. If you see **Germinal Centers**, it is a **Secondary** lymphoid organ (B-cells are reacting to something).
2. If you see **Hassall’s Corpuscles**, it’s the **Thymus**.
3. If you see **Cords of Billroth** or **Red Pulp**, it’s the **Spleen**.
4. Only **Lymph Nodes** have afferent lymphatic vessels (lymph flows *into* them).

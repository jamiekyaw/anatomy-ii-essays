> Spleen. Macroscopic and microscopic characteristics. Blood and nerve supply.

---

# 1. Overview
- **Definition:** The largest lymphoid organ in the body.
- **Location:** Left hypochondrium (Left Upper Quadrant), tucked between the fundus of the stomach and the diaphragm.

---

# 2. Macroscopic Anatomy
The spleen has two surfaces, two borders, and two poles.

**Surfaces:**
- **Diaphragmatic Surface:**
	- Smooth and convex; fits against the diaphragm.
- **Visceral Surface:**
	- Concave and irregular; related to surrounding organs.

**Borders:**
- **Superior Border:** Characteristically **notched**.
- **Inferior Border:** Rounded and smooth.

**Visceral Relations (Impressions):**
- **Gastric Impression:** For the stomach.
- **Renal Impression:** For the left kidney.
- **Colic Impression:** For the splenic flexure of the colon.
- **Pancreatic Impression:** For the **tail of the pancreas** (located at the hilum).
- **The Hilum:** A fissure on the visceral surface where the splenic artery enters and the splenic vein exits.

**Peritoneal Ligaments:**
- **Gastrosplenic Ligament:** Connects spleen to stomach.
- **Splenorenal (Lienorenal) Ligament:** Connects spleen to left kidney (contains **splenic artery/vein** and **tail of pancreas**).

# 3. Microscopic Anatomy
The spleen does not have a cortex or medulla; it is composed of **Splenic Pulp** enclosed in a fibroelastic capsule.

- **Capsule and Trabeculae:** A dense connective tissue capsule sends "trabeculae" (beams) into the organ to provide a framework.
- **White Pulp (Immune Function):**
    - **PALS (Periarteriolar Lymphoid Sheaths):** Collection of **T-cells** surrounding the central artery.
    - **Splenic Nodules (Malpighian Corpuscles):** Contain **B-cells** (germinal centers).
- **Red Pulp (Filtration Function):**
    - Makes up the bulk of the organ.
    - **Splenic Sinusoids:** Large, leaky blood vessels.
    - **Splenic Cords (Cords of Billroth):** Tissue containing macrophages that remove old/damaged Red Blood Cells (RBCs).
- **Marginal Zone:** The boundary between white and red pulp where antigen-presenting cells reside.

# 4. Blood Supply
The spleen is highly vascular, receiving about 5% of total cardiac output.

- **Arterial Supply:**
    - **Splenic Artery:** A branch of the **Celiac Trunk**.
    - It is highly **tortuous** (corkscrew shape) to allow for splenic expansion and movement of the diaphragm.
    - It travels within the splenorenal ligament.
- **Venous Drainage:**
    - **Splenic Vein:** Runs a straight course behind the pancreas.
    - It joins the **Superior Mesenteric Vein (SMV)** behind the neck of the pancreas to form the **Hepatic Portal Vein**.

---

# 5. Nerve Supply
- **Source:** **Celiac Plexus**.
- **Fiber types:** Mainly **Sympathetic** postganglionic fibers.
- **Function:** Vasomotor (regulates blood flow). There is no significant parasympathetic supply to the splenic parenchyma. The nerves follow the splenic artery to enter the hilum.

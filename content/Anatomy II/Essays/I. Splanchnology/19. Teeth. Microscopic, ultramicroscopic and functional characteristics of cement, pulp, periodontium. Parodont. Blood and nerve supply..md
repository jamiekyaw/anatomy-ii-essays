> Teeth. Microscopic, ultramicroscopic and functional characteristics of cement, pulp, periodontium. Parodont. Blood and nerve supply.

---

# 1. Cementum (Cement)
Mineralized connective tissue covering the tooth root.
- **Composition:**
	- 45-50% Hydroxyapatite
	- 50% Collagen Type I
	+ Water
- **Microscopic Types:**
    - **Acellular:**
	    - Covers upper 2/3 of root.
	    - No cells trapped inside.
    - **Cellular:**
	    - Found at apical 1/3 (root tip).
	    - Contains **Cementocytes** residing in spaces called **Lacunae**.
- **Ultramicroscopic:**
	- Hydroxyapatite crystals associated with collagen fibers.
	- **Sharpey’s Fibers** (PDL fibers) are embedded to anchor the tooth.
- **Functions:**
    - Anchors Periodontal Ligament (PDL) fibers to the root.
    - Protects the underlying dentin.

# 2. Dental Pulp
The only soft tissue of the tooth, located in the central pulp chamber.
- **Microscopic Zones (Outer to Inner):**
    - **Odontoblastic Layer:**
	    - Line the periphery.
	    - Produces dentin.
    - **Cell-Free Zone (Zone of Weil):**
	    - Contains capillaries and nerve plexuses.
    - **Cell-Rich Zone:**
	    - High density of fibroblasts.
	    - Undifferentiated mesenchymal cells (stem cells).
    - **Pulp Core:**
	    - Central mass containing large blood vessels and nerves.
- **Cells:** Odontoblasts, Fibroblasts, Macrophages, Stem cells.
- **Functions:**
    - **Formative:** Produces dentin throughout life.
    - **Nutritive:** Supplies nutrients to mineralized tissues.
    - **Sensory:** Transmits pain (only sensation pulp can feel).
    - **Protective:** Forms reparative dentin in response to injury/decay.

# 3. Periodontium & Parodont
**Parodont** is simply the clinical term for the **Periodontium** (the functional system of tissues supporting the tooth).

**The 4 Components:**
1. **Gingiva:** The "gums."
2. **Periodontal Ligament (PDL):** Connective tissue between cementum and bone.
3. **Cementum**
4. **Alveolar Bone**

**Periodontal Ligament (PDL) specifics:**
- **Microscopic:**
	- Dense collagen fiber bundles (Principal Fibers) arranged to resist forces.
- **Cell types:**
	- Fibroblasts
	- Osteoblasts
	- Cementoblasts
	- **Epithelial Rests of Malassez** (remnants of tooth development)
- **Functions:** 
    - **Physical:** Shock absorber for chewing forces.
    - **Sensory:** Proprioception (detects pressure/where the tooth is in space).

# 4. Blood Supply
- **Main Source:** Maxillary Artery (branch of External Carotid).
- **Specific Paths:**
    - **Upper Teeth:** Superior Alveolar Arteries (Posterior, Middle, Anterior).
    - **Lower Teeth:** Inferior Alveolar Artery (runs in the mandibular canal).
- **Distribution:**
    - **Dental Artery:** Enters the tooth via the Apical Foramen to supply the pulp.
    - **Interalveolar/Alveolar Arteries:** Supply the PDL and bone.

---

# 5. Nerve Supply
- **Main Source:** Trigeminal Nerve (Cranial Nerve V).
- **Maxillary (V2):** Superior Alveolar Nerves supply upper teeth.
- **Mandibular (V3):** Inferior Alveolar Nerve supplies lower teeth.
- **Function in Pulp:** Only contains **A-delta** (sharp pain) and **C-fibers** (dull, aching pain).
- **Function in PDL:** Contains **Mechanoreceptors** (sense of touch/pressure during biting).

# Summary for Quick Revision
- **Cementum:** Acellular vs. Cellular; Sharpey's fibers.
- **Pulp:** 4 layers; Odontoblasts; Pain is the only sensation.
- **Periodontium:** PDL, Bone, Cementum, Gingiva.
- **Vessels:** Maxillary Artery $\rightarrow$ Alveolar branches.
- **Nerves:** CN V (Trigeminal) $\rightarrow$ V2 (Upper) and V3 (Lower).

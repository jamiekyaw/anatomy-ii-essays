> Aorta. Microscopic, ultramicroscopic and functional characteristics of its wall.

# 1. Classification
The aorta is the primary example of a **Large Elastic Artery** (also known as a **Conducting Artery**). Its main role is to receive blood under high pressure from the heart and ensure continuous flow to the periphery.

---

# 2. Microscopic Structure (Light Microscopy)
The wall of the aorta is composed of three distinct layers (tunics):

![](https://medcell.org/systems_cell_biology/blood_vessels_lab/images/aorta_wall_labels.jpg)

## A. Tunica Intima (Innermost)
- **Endothelium:** A single layer of simple squamous epithelial cells.
- **Subendothelial Layer:** Loose connective tissue containing occasional smooth muscle cells and collagen.
- **Internal Elastic Lamina:** In the aorta, this is **thin and indistinct** because it blends in with the elastic lamellae of the media.

## B. Tunica Media (Thickest Layer)
- This is the most prominent layer, designed to withstand high systolic pressure.
- **Elastic Lamellae:** Consists of 40–70 sheets of **fenestrated elastic fibers** (circularly arranged). The "fenestrations" (holes) allow nutrients to diffuse through the wall.
- **Smooth Muscle Cells (SMCs):** Located between the elastic sheets. 
    - *Unique Fact:* In the aorta, there are **no fibroblasts** in the media. The SMCs are responsible for secreting the collagen, elastin, and ground substance.
- **External Elastic Lamina:** Indistinct; it is simply the last layer of the media.

## C. Tunica Adventitia (Outermost)
- Relatively thin compared to the media.
- Composed of collagen and elastic fibers (longitudinally arranged) to prevent over-expansion.
- **Vasa Vasorum:** "Vessels of the vessels." Because the aortic wall is so thick, oxygen cannot diffuse from the lumen to the outer layers; these small blood vessels supply the adventitia and outer media.
- **Nervi Vasorum:** Autonomic nerves controlling vasomotor tone.

---

# 3. Ultramicroscopic Characteristics (Electron Microscopy)
To get top marks, mention these specific organelles and structures:

- **Endothelial Cells:**
    - **Weibel-Palade Bodies:** Rod-like cytoplasmic inclusions that store **von Willebrand Factor** (for blood clotting) and **P-selectin**.
    - **Tight Junctions (Zonula Occludens):** Prevent leakage of plasma into the wall.
    - **Pinocytotic Vesicles:** Transport substances across the blood-arterial barrier.
- **Elastic Fibers:**
    - Show a central core of **elastin** protein surrounded by a network of **fibrillin microfibrils**.
- **Smooth Muscle Cells:**
    - Show an abundance of Rough Endoplasmic Reticulum (RER) and Golgi apparatus (because they are "synthetic" cells producing the matrix).
    - Attached to elastic lamellae via basement membrane anchors to help distribute mechanical stress.

# 4. Functional Characteristics

## A. The Windkessel Effect (Blood Pressure Reservoir)
- **During systole (when the heart squeezes):** Blood is pumped into the aorta, causing it to stretch and expand. The aorta's elastic walls store some of this energy.
- **During diastole (when the heart relaxes):** The stretched aorta springs back to its original size. This pushes blood forward, helping maintain a steady flow of blood to the body even when the heart is not actively pumping.

## B. High Compliance
- The aorta is "compliant".
	- It can increase its volume significantly without a massive increase in pressure
	- It protects smaller downstream vessels from bursting.

## C. Nutrient Diffusion
- The **fenestrations** in the tunica media are functional "windows".
	- Allow oxygen and nutrients to reach deep-seated smooth muscle cells.

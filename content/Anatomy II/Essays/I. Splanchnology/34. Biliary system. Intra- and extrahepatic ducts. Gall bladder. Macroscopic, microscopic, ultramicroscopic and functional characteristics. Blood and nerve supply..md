> Biliary system. Intra- and extrahepatic ducts. Gall bladder. Macroscopic, microscopic, ultramicroscopic and functional characteristics. Blood and nerve supply.

---

# 1. Overview
The biliary system transports bile from the liver to the duodenum.
- **Intrahepatic:** Inside the liver.
- **Extrahepatic:** Outside the liver.

---

# 2. Intrahepatic Ducts (The Path)
1. **Bile Canaliculi:** Tiny channels formed by grooves between adjacent hepatocytes.
2. **Canals of Hering:** Short terminals connecting canaliculi to bile ducts.
3. **Interlobular Bile Ducts:** Located in the **Portal Triads**.

- **Microscopic:** Lined by **simple cuboidal epithelium**.
- **Ultramicroscopic:** Microvilli on the luminal surface; tight junctions between cells to prevent bile leakage into blood.
- **Function:** Collect bile; secrete water and bicarbonate.

# 3. Extrahepatic Ducts (The Anatomy)
1. **Right & Left Hepatic Ducts** exit the liver.
2. Join to form the **Common Hepatic Duct**.
3. Joined by the **Cystic Duct** (from Gallbladder) to form the **Common Bile Duct**.
4. CBD joins the **Main Pancreatic Duct** at the **Ampulla of Vater**.
5. Enters the duodenum at the **Major Duodenal Papilla**.

- **Sphincter of Oddi:** Smooth muscle valve controlling the flow of bile/pancreatic juice into the duodenum.
- **Microscopic:** Simple columnar epithelium with a thick fibrous wall containing smooth muscle.

# 4. Gallbladder (GB)
**Macroscopic:** Pear-shaped sac (7-10cm) on the inferior surface of the liver.
- **Parts:** Fundus, Body, Neck.

**Microscopic (Layers):**
1. **Mucosa:** Simple columnar epithelium with microvilli. Folded into **rugae** (disappear when distended). *Note: No muscularis mucosae or submucosa.*
2. **Muscularis:** Smooth muscle fibers in random orientations.
3. **Adventitia/Serosa:** Adventitia (where it touches the liver) or Serosa (peritoneum).

**Ultramicroscopic:**
- Numerous mitochondria.
- Lateral intercellular spaces.

**Functional Characteristics:**
1. **Storage:** Holds bile.
2. **Concentration:** Absorbs water and electrolytes (concentrates bile up to 10x).
3. **Expulsion:** Contracts to release bile into the duodenum.

---

# 5. Functional Regulation (How it works)
- **Hormonal:** Fat in the duodenum triggers **Cholecystokinin (CCK)** release. CCK causes GB contraction and relaxation of the Sphincter of Oddi.
- **Neural:** Vagus nerve stimulates contraction (minor).

---

# 6. Blood and Nerve Supply
**Blood Supply:**
- **Arterial:** **Cystic Artery**.
- **Venous:** **Cystic Veins** (drain into the Portal Vein).

**Nerve Supply:**
- **Parasympathetic:** Vagus Nerve (CN X) – causes contraction.
- **Sympathetic:** Celiac Plexus – inhibits contraction (vasoconstriction).
- **Sensory:** Phrenic nerve (referred pain from GB can be felt in the right shoulder).

---

# Summary Checklist for the Exam:
- **Duct flow:** Canaliculi $\rightarrow$ Hering $\rightarrow$ Interlobular $\rightarrow$ Hepatic $\rightarrow$ Cystic/Common Bile $\rightarrow$ Duodenum.
- **Cell type:** Cholangiocytes (Cuboidal in small ducts, Columnar in large).
- **GB Layering:** No submucosa.
- **Concentration:** GB's main job (uses active transport).
- **Trigger:** CCK (Fatty meal).
- **Artery:** Cystic (found in the Cystohepatic Triangle of Calot).

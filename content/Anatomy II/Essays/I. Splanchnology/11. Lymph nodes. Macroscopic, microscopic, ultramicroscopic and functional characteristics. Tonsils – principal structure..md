> Lymph nodes. Macroscopic, microscopic, ultramicroscopic and functional characteristics. Tonsils – principal structure.

# I. Lymph Nodes

Lymph nodes are small, encapsulated bean-shaped organs located along the pathway of lymphatic vessels. They serve as biological filters.

### 1. Macroscopic Characteristics
- **Shape:** Kidney or bean-shaped.
- **Size:** Varies from 1 mm to 20 mm.
- **External Features:**
    - **Convex Surface:** Where multiple **afferent** lymphatic vessels enter.
    - **Concave Surface (Hilum):** Where the **efferent** lymphatic vessel leaves, and blood vessels (artery/vein) and nerves enter/exit.
- **Color:** Usually greyish-pink (may be black in lungs due to carbon/anthracosis).

### 2. Microscopic Characteristics (Histology)
The lymph node is divided into a **stroma** (framework) and **parenchyma** (functional tissue).

**A. Stroma (The Support)**
- **Capsule:** Dense irregular connective tissue surrounding the node.
- **Trabeculae:** Connective tissue partitions extending from the capsule into the node.
- **Reticular Meshwork:** A fine veil of Reticular fibers (Type III collagen) and reticular cells that support the lymphocytes.

**B. Parenchyma (The Functional Zones)**
1. **Outer Cortex:** Contains **Lymphatic Follicles (Nodules)**.
    - *Primary Follicles:* Uniformly dark (inactive).
    - *Secondary Follicles:* Contain a **Germinal Center** (pale center where B-cells proliferate) and a **Mantle zone** (darker outer ring).
2. **Paracortex (Deep Cortex):** The "Thymus-dependent zone." It lacks follicles and is rich in **T-lymphocytes**.
3. **Medulla:** Inner region organized into:
    - **Medullary Cords:** Columns of B-cells, plasma cells, and macrophages.
    - **Medullary Sinuses:** Dilated spaces separating the cords.

### 3. Ultramicroscopic Characteristics (Electron Microscopy)
- **Reticular Cells:** Large, star-shaped cells that produce Type III collagen.
- **Follicular Dendritic Cells (FDCs):** Have long cytoplasmic processes that "trap" antigens to present to B-cells.
- **High Endothelial Venules (HEVs):** Characterized by cuboidal (rather than flat) endothelial cells with specialized receptors (selectins) for lymphocyte "homing."

![|500](https://www.cell.com/cms/10.1016/j.trecan.2020.10.001/asset/ec59bca1-6612-42c2-b32b-cee5c65f70fe/main.assets/gr1_lrg.jpg)

### 4. Functional Characteristics
- **Filtration:** Macrophages in the sinuses phagocytose foreign particles, bacteria, and cell debris.
- **B-Cell Proliferation:** Occurs in the germinal centers of the cortex (humoral immunity).
- **T-Cell Activation:** Occurs in the paracortex (cell-mediated immunity).
- **Antibody Production:** Plasma cells in the medullary cords secrete antibodies into the lymph.

# II. Tonsils

Tonsils are collection of unencapsulated or partially encapsulated lymphoid tissue located in the mucosa of the pharynx (**MALT** - Mucosa-Associated Lymphoid Tissue).

### 1. Waldeyer’s Ring
The tonsils form a protective ring at the entrance of the digestive and respiratory tracts:
- **Palatine Tonsils:** (The "true" tonsils) Located in the oropharynx.
- **Pharyngeal Tonsil:** (Adenoids) Located in the nasopharynx.
- **Lingual Tonsils:** Located at the base of the tongue.

![](https://dentalsleeppractice.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/800x400_Colquitt.jpg)

### 2. Principal Structure (Focus: Palatine Tonsil)
The Palatine tonsil is the most commonly studied model:

- **Epithelium:** Covered by **Stratified Squamous Non-Keratinized Epithelium**.
- **Tonsillar Crypts:** The epithelium invaginates deeply into the parenchyma to form 10–20 deep pits called crypts. These trap bacteria and food particles to "sample" antigens.
- **Lymphoid Follicles:** Numerous secondary follicles (with germinal centers) line the sides of the crypts.
- **Hemicapsule:** A thick band of dense connective tissue at the **base** of the tonsil. It acts as a barrier against the spread of infection.
- **Absence of Afferent Lymphatics:** Unlike lymph nodes, tonsils **do not** have afferent lymphatic vessels. They only have efferent vessels for drainage.

### 3. Key Differences for Exams
| Feature | Lymph Node | Tonsil |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Afferent Lymphatics** | Present | Absent |
| **Epithelium** | None (Encapsulated) | Stratified Squamous/Respiratory |
| **Surface Feature** | Smooth Capsule | Tonsillar Crypts |
| **Cortex/Medulla** | Clearly defined | Not distinct |

# Summary for Quick Revision
1. **Lymph Flow:** Remember the order: Afferent $\rightarrow$ Subcapsular $\rightarrow$ Trabecular $\rightarrow$ Medullary $\rightarrow$ Efferent.
2. **Cell Zones:** Cortex = B-cells; Paracortex = T-cells.
3. **Tonsil Identification:** If you see "Crypts" and "Stratified Squamous Epithelium" on a slide, it is a Palatine Tonsil.

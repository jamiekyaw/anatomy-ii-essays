> Nose, nasal cavity and paranasal air sinuses. Macroscopic structure. Respiratory and olfactory parts of the nasal mucosa - microscopic, ultramicroscopic and functional characteristics. Blood and nerve supply.

---

# 1. Macroscopic Structure

## External Nose
- **Framework:** Superior part is **bone** (nasal, frontal, maxillary). Inferior part is **hyaline cartilage** (septal, lateral, and alar cartilages).
- **Nares:** The nostrils; entry point.

## Nasal Cavity
- **Septum:** Divides the cavity. Formed by Vomer, Ethmoid, and Septal cartilage.
- **Lateral Wall:** Characterized by three projections called **Conchae** (Superior, Middle, Inferior).
- **Meatuses:** Spaces below each concha.
    - **Inferior meatus**
    - **Middle meatus**
    - **Superior meatus**
    - **Spheno-ethmoidal recess**
- **Choanae:** Posterior openings leading into the nasopharynx.

## Paranasal Air Sinuses
- Air-filled cavities in bones lined with respiratory mucosa.
- **Four pairs:** Frontal, Maxillary, Ethmoidal, and Sphenoid.
- **Functions:** Lighten the skull, resonate voice, humidify air.

# 2. Nasal Mucosa (Microscopic & Functional)

The cavity is divided into the **Vestibule** (skin/hairs), **Respiratory region**, and **Olfactory region**.

## A. Respiratory Mucosa (Lower 2/3)
- **Microscopic:** Pseudostratified ciliated columnar epithelium with **Goblet cells**.
- **Ultramicroscopic:** 
    - Cilia to move mucus.
    - Microvilli to increase surface area.
    - Basement membrane is thick.
- **Functional:**
    - **Warming:** Rich venous plexuses heat the air.
    - **Moistening:** Goblet cells and seromucous glands provide moisture.
    - **Cleaning:** Mucus traps dust; cilia move it toward the pharynx.

## B. Olfactory Mucosa (Upper 1/3 - Roof & Superior Concha)
- **Microscopic:** Thicker, yellowish epithelium. No goblet cells. 
- **Cell Types:**
    1. **Olfactory Neurons**
    2. **Sustentacular Cells:** Provide metabolic support.
    3. **Basal Cells**
- **Ultramicroscopic:** 
    - Olfactory vesicles with **non-motile cilia** containing odorant receptors.
    - **Bowman’s Glands:** Secrete serous fluid to dissolve odorants.
- **Functional:** Detection of smell (olfaction).

# 3. Blood Supply

- **Arterial Supply:**
    1. **Sphenopalatine artery**
    2. **Anterior & Posterior Ethmoidal arteries**
    3. **Greater Palatine** and **Superior Labial** arteries
- **Kiesselbach’s Plexus (Little’s Area)**
- **Venous Drainage:** Drains into the Facial vein and the Pterygoid venous plexus.

---

# 4. Nerve Supply

- **Special Sensory (Smell):** Olfactory Nerve (CN I) via the olfactory bulbs.
- **General Sensory (Touch/Pain):** 
    - **V1 (Ophthalmic Nerve)**
    - **V2 (Maxillary Nerve)**
- **Autonomics:** 
    - **Parasympathetic:** From Facial Nerve (CN VII).
    - **Sympathetic:** From T1 spinal level (causes vasoconstriction to reduce swelling).

---

# Summary Checklist for the Exam:
- **Lateral Wall:** Remember "3 Conchae, 3 Meatuses."
- **Histology:** Respiratory = Cilia/Goblet; Olfactory = Bipolar neurons/Bowman's glands.
- **Epistaxis:** Little’s Area / Kiesselbach’s Plexus.
- **Sinus drainage:** Most drain into the **Middle Meatus**.

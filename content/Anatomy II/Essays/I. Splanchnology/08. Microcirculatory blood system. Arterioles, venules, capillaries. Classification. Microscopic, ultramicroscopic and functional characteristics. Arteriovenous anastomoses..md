
> Microcirculatory blood system. Arterioles, venules, capillaries. Classification. Microscopic, ultramicroscopic and functional characteristics. Arteriovenous anastomoses.

---

# 1. Definition and Overview
Microcirculation refers to the flow of blood through the smallest vessels (diameter <100 $\mu$m) in the body.

It is the site where the primary functions of the circulatory system—**exchange of gases, nutrients, and waste**—actually occur.

**Components:**
1. **Arterioles** (Resistance vessels)
2. **Metarterioles** (Regulatory vessels)
3. **Capillaries** (Exchange vessels)
4. **Post-capillary Venules** (Collection vessels)
5. **Arteriovenous Anastomoses** (Shunts)

# 2. Arterioles (Resistance Vessels)
Arterioles are the final branches of the arterial system that lead into capillary beds.

- **Microscopic Structure:**
    - **Tunica Intima:** Endothelium and a very thin subendothelial layer.
    - **Tunica Media:** 1 to 2 layers of smooth muscle cells arranged circularly.
    - **Tunica Adventitia:** Thin, merges with surrounding connective tissue.
- **Ultramicroscopic Features:**
    - Endothelial cells are linked by gap junctions and tight junctions.
    - Presence of **myoendothelial junctions** where endothelial cells touch smooth muscle cells to communicate.
- **Functional Characteristics:**
    - **Peripheral Resistance:** They are the primary site of vascular resistance. By contracting or relaxing, they regulate systemic blood pressure.
    - **Flow Regulation:** Act as "stopcocks" to control how much blood enters a specific organ.

# 3. Capillaries (Exchange Vessels)
The smallest vessels (5–10 $\mu$m diameter), just wide enough for a single red blood cell to pass.

### General Structure
- Consist only of **endothelium** and a **basal lamina**.
- **Pericytes:** Contractile cells wrapped around capillaries that help stabilize the wall and assist in repair.

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/7c/Capillary.svg)

## Classification
1. **Continuous Capillaries:**
    - *Location:* Muscle, lungs, CNS (Blood-Brain Barrier), exocrine glands.
    - *Structure:* Uninterrupted lining. Cells held together by tight junctions (occluding junctions).
    - *Function:* Highly selective transport via **pinocytotic vesicles**.
2. **Fenestrated Capillaries:**
    - *Location:* Kidney (glomerulus), endocrine glands, intestinal mucosa.
    - *Structure:* Endothelial cells have small pores (**fenestrae**) covered by a thin diaphragm.
    - *Function:* Rapid exchange of water and larger molecules.
3. **Sinusoidal (Discontinuous) Capillaries:**
    - *Location:* Liver, spleen, bone marrow.
    - *Structure:* Large diameter, large gaps between cells, and a **discontinuous or absent basal lamina**.
    - *Function:* Allows passage of whole cells (blood cells) and large proteins.

# 4. Venules
### A. Post-capillary Venules
- **Structure:** Endothelium supported by pericytes; no true smooth muscle yet.
- **Function:**
	- This is the primary site of **diapedesis** (leukocytes leaving the blood) during inflammation.
	- They are very sensitive to histamine and serotonin.

### B. Muscular Venules
- **Structure:** Contain 1–2 layers of smooth muscle.
- **Function:**
	- Act as a reservoir for blood (capacitance vessels) and lead into larger veins.

---

# 5. Metarterioles and Pre-capillary Sphincters
- **Metarteriole:** A "thoroughfare channel" that connects an arteriole directly to a venule.
- **Pre-capillary Sphincter:** A ring of smooth muscle at the origin of a capillary from a metarteriole.
- **Function:** They "pulse" (vasomotion). If the tissue doesn't need blood, the sphincters close, and blood bypasses the capillary bed through the metarteriole.

# 6. Arteriovenous Anastomoses (AVAs)
Arteriovenous Anastomoses are direct shortcuts between arterioles and venules that bypass the capillary bed entirely.

- **Microscopic Structure:**
    - Possess a thick, highly muscular tunica media.
    - Richly innervated by the autonomic nervous system.
- **Functional Characteristics:**
    - **Thermoregulation:** Found heavily in the skin (fingertips, nose, ears). 
	    - When open, they shunt blood to the surface to lose heat
	    - When closed, they conserve heat by keeping blood in deeper vessels.
    - **Pressure Regulation:** Can quickly redistribute blood volume.
